A DiT is a standard transformer whose tokens are noisy latent patches and whose output is a denoising target; making it causal — generating in chunks that attend only to finished chunks via a KV cache — is what turns a video generator into something that can run a control loop.

Intuition

Three renames turn a language model into a video diffusion model. Words become latent patches (compressed image pieces); next-token prediction becomes denoising (predict the flow-matching velocity for every noisy token — see Flow Matching); and the decoder-only causal mask becomes block-causal over chunks: a chunk of frames being denoised may read every finished chunk, never the future. Everything else — attention, MLPs, residuals, KV caching — is the same machinery LLMs use.

Latents first: why nothing here touches pixels

A 480P frame is ~400k pixels, mostly texture that carries no scene information. Video models therefore run every frame through a VAE and do all generation on the compressed latents; pixels appear only at the ends. The compression is spatial and temporal — in DreamZero’s Wan2.1 backbone, 8 latent frames stand in for 33 raw frames (6.6 s at 5 FPS). When a WAM paper says “video”, read “a small grid of latent vectors”.

Inside the VAE: how a 3-D convolution becomes causal — with the dimensions

A 3-D convolution is an ordinary convolution whose kernel spans time as well as space, (kt,kh,kw)(k_t, k_h, k_w)(kt​,kh​,kw​) — say 3×3×33 \times 3 \times 33×3×3: each output value summarizes a small spatiotemporal neighborhood. The catch is where that temporal window sits. Centered (the default), the output at time τ\tauτ reads inputs at τ−1,τ,τ+1\tau - 1, \tau, \tau + 1τ−1,τ,τ+1 — it sees the future. Making it causal is one shift, not new machinery:

Definition — Causal temporal convolution

A convolution over time whose output at step τ\tauτ depends only on inputs at steps ≤τ\le \tau≤τ. Implementation: pad kt−1k_t - 1kt​−1 frames on the past side and none on the future side, so the kernel window ends at the present. Stacking layers grows the receptive field backward only (the WaveNet construction, applied here to video).

Read Fig. DIT-3 top-to-bottom, then the numbers. Every edge leans backward, so no output ever depends on a later frame; strided causal stages do the temporal downsampling (each stride-2 stage halves time — two of them give the ÷4), and strides in h,wh, wh,w give the spatial ÷8. The accent path is the anchor: with only past-side padding behind it, frame 1’s latent is computed from frame 1 alone — which is exactly what lets an image-to-video model condition on a real frame and lets the encoder run on a growing stream (nothing already encoded ever needs revisiting when new frames arrive).

Now “a small grid of vectors”, made concrete with Wan2.1’s public configuration (the DreamZero paper itself states only the 8 ↔ 33 correspondence): the latent has C=16C = 16C=16 channels, so one 832 × 480 frame becomes a 60×10460 \times 10460×104 grid — 480 ÷ 8 by 832 ÷ 8 — holding a 16-dimensional vector in each cell: shape 16×60×10416 \times 60 \times 10416×60×104, 99,840 numbers where 4 raw frames held ~4.79 M (≈ 48× compression; positions shrink 4 × 8 × 8 = 256× while channels grow 3 → 16). The DiT does not eat that grid whole: patchify with patch size 1 × 2 × 2 turns each 2 × 2 spatial tile (16 × 2 × 2 = 64 numbers) into one token via a linear projection to the model width d=5120d = 5120d=5120 — 30 × 52 = 1,560 tokens per latent frame, so a K = 2 chunk carries ≈ 3,120 video tokens (single-view arithmetic; DreamZero tiles its three camera views into one frame, exact layout Not reported). And the reconciliation the arithmetic demands: the 8 chunked latent frames each summarize 4 raw frames while the anchor rides alongside as the conditioning frame — 1 + 8 × 4 = 33.

The DiT block

Chop the latent grid into patches, project each to a fixed-width vector (a token), and stack the standard loop: attention (every token reads a weighted mix of the others), MLP, residuals. Two things make it a diffusion transformer: tokens are noisy latents, and the timestep ttt enters every block by scaling and shifting the normalization layers (the adaLN recipe of Peebles & Xie 2023) — one network thereby serves every noise level:

Two readings of the drawing worth making explicit. First, plain LN uses a fixed, learned scale and shift; the adaptive version replaces those constants with functions of the conditioning — that is the entire trick:

where SubBlock is self-attention or the MLP. In adaLN-Zero the gates alpha\\alphaalpha are initialized to zero, so every block starts as the identity and training grows the computation gradually.

Second, the timestep’s full route: the scalar ttt is encoded as sinusoidal features (smooth sin/cos at many frequencies — the same trick as positional encodings, so nearby noise levels get nearby codes), an MLP maps those to one conditioning vector ccc, and each block’s small head maps ccc to its own six signals (gamma1,beta1,alpha1,gamma2,beta2,alpha2)(\\gamma_1, \\beta_1, \\alpha_1, \\gamma_2, \\beta_2, \\alpha_2)(gamma1​,beta1​,alpha1​,gamma2​,beta2​,alpha2​). The timestep is never a token in this recipe: it acts multiplicatively and additively on every block’s activations, which is how one set of weights can behave like a family of denoisers — one per noise level.

Why scaling-and-shifting, of all things?

The intention becomes clear once you say what the network must be: one set of weights acting as a whole family of denoisers. At high noise (small ttt) the useful computation is coarse — guess global layout from almost nothing; at low noise it is fine — sharpen details without moving structure. So the noise level must be able to re-tune what every layer computes, including the last ones. Compare the three standard ways to inject it:

  • As an extra token? ttt is global — every token in the chunk shares the same value — so attention would spend capacity re-fetching one constant fact, block after block.
  • Concatenated once at the input? Forty blocks later the signal has washed out, and the late blocks are precisely the ones whose behavior must differ most across noise levels.
  • Feature-wise modulation at every block (the FiLM pattern, which adaLN instantiates): a per-channel scale can amplify, suppress, or flip a feature — it reconfigures each block’s computation — for the price of a few thousand numbers per block, applied uniformly to all tokens, exactly matching the global nature of the information.

The normalization layer is the natural injection site because LN has just stripped each token’s scale and shift — re-introducing them as functions of the conditioning costs nothing structurally and keeps activations well-ranged. The zero-initialized gates finish the story: every residual branch starts closed, each block begins as the identity, and training opens the gates gradually — which is what makes a 40-block denoiser stable to train. This is not just taste: the DiT paper ablated in-context token conditioning, cross-attention, and adaLN variants, and adaLN-Zero won on both quality and compute (Peebles & Xie 2023).

Why sinusoidal features, and not the raw scalar?

Definition — Sinusoidal (Fourier) features

The scalar ttt is encoded as its readings on a bank of sinusoids at geometrically spaced frequencies,

e(t)  =  [ sin⁡(ω1t), cos⁡(ω1t), …, sin⁡(ωKt), cos⁡(ωKt) ],ωk+1=r ωke(t) \;=\; \bigl[\, \sin(\omega_1 t),\ \cos(\omega_1 t),\ \ldots,\ \sin(\omega_K t),\ \cos(\omega_K t) \,\bigr], \qquad \omega_{k+1} = r\,\omega_ke(t)=[sin(ω1​t), cos(ω1​t), …, sin(ωK​t), cos(ωK​t)],ωk+1​=rωk​

— the same construction as transformer positional encodings, standard in diffusion models since DDPM.

Why bother, when ttt is a perfectly good number? Because a plain MLP fed one raw scalar is biased toward overly smooth functions of it (the spectral-bias result — Fourier features are the standard cure), while a denoiser’s behavior genuinely must change quickly in places — most sharply near the ends of the schedule. The bank fixes this at every scale at once: slow waves encode early-versus-late, fast waves separate neighboring timesteps, nearby ttt always map to nearby vectors (no binning cliffs), and the code has constant norm with rich linear structure that the downstream MLP and per-block heads can slice by frequency. One scalar in, a whole spectrum of handles out.

Attention over one shared sequence is also what makes multimodal joint modeling real rather than a slogan: video tokens, action tokens, text tokens and state tokens can sit in the same sequence, so (in a WAM) action tokens read the imagined future directly at every block. Nothing in the trunk is modality- or robot-specific — which is precisely why a video-pretrained trunk transfers.

Making it causal: chunks, KV cache, teacher forcing

A stock video DiT is bidirectional: it emits one fixed-length clip, every frame attending to every other. Two properties disqualify that for control: you need actions now, not after the clip; and fixed windows force frame subsampling that breaks alignment between video time and action time. The causal variant generates chunk by chunk — in DreamZero, a chunk is K=2K=2K=2 latent frames paired with H=48H=48H=48 actions, 1.6 s of world — under a block-causal mask: the chunk being denoised attends to all finished chunks and to itself, never the future. Finished chunks persist as a KV cache, so context grows almost for free.

Definition — Block-causal attention mask

Group the sequence into chunks 1,…,M1, \dots, M1,…,M. A query token in chunk iii may attend to a key token in chunk jjj iff j<ij < ij<i (finished, clean context) or j=ij = ij=i (its own window, bidirectional). Conditioning tokens (text, proprioception) are attendable from every chunk.

What does “K = 2 latent frames paired with H = 48 actions, 1.6 s of world” mean concretely? Three clocks describing the same physical time span — and yes: the 48 actions are denoised at once, as one action chunk, then streamed sequentially by the 30 Hz controller over the next 1.6 s while the GPU denoises the following chunk. The numbers lock together: 1.6 s × 5 FPS = 8 raw frames; 8 raw frames ÷ 4 (temporal VAE compression) = 2 latent frames; 1.6 s × 30 Hz = 48 actions. Autoregression lives at chunk granularity only — never action-by-action.

Training uses teacher forcing: previous chunks in context are clean ground truth. At inference a pure video generator must feed on its own imperfect outputs, and the mismatch compounds — autoregressive drift. A closed-loop robot model has an exit no offline generator has: after each chunk executes, the real camera frames can overwrite the predicted frames in the KV cache, restoring teacher-forcing conditions every chunk (DreamZero, §3.1 — toggle it in Fig. DIT-2’s inference tab). Cousin fixes in the offline world — noise injection on context, diffusion forcing — only soften the mismatch; observation overwrite deletes it, at the price of needing an environment that answers back.

From velocity to a finished chunk: who actually denoises?

A DiT block denoises nothing by itself — and neither does the full stack, in a single call. One forward pass reads the noisy chunk (plus context and conditioning) and outputs a velocity for every token: the flow-matching arrow saying “move this way” (see Flow Matching, Fig. FM-1’s field view). Why does chunk-causal generation matter for a robot, beyond latency?
Alignment and statefulness. Bidirectional models need fixed-length windows, which forces video subsampling that distorts the correspondence between frame time and action time; causal chunks preserve native rates. And the KV cache is a visual memory: each new chunk conditions on up to 6.6 s of actual history — at inference, real observed history, thanks to the overwrite.
Where exactly does the LLM analogy break?
Two places. Tokens within a chunk are denoised jointly with bidirectional attention among themselves (an LLM is causal token-by-token), and the model predicts continuous velocities for latent patches rather than a softmax over a vocabulary. The causal structure lives at chunk granularity, not token granularity.
Why do 8 latent frames correspond to 33 raw frames rather than 32?
Because the VAE is causal with an exact anchor: the first raw frame is encoded alone as the conditioning frame, and each of the 8 chunked latent frames summarizes 4 raw frames — 1 + 8 × 4 = 33, with the anchor riding alongside the 8 rather than being one of them. The anchor is what makes image-to-video conditioning exact and streaming encoding possible; a plain non-causal 4× VAE would give the rounder 32.
Why is t injected at all 40 blocks via scale-and-shift, rather than fed once as an input or a token?
Because t must reconfigure the whole family of computations: early blocks and late blocks both change behavior with noise level, and a signal injected once at the bottom washes out by the top. As a token it would waste attention on a value every token already shares. Per-block FiLM-style scales and shifts can amplify, suppress or flip each feature channel — reconfiguring every block for a few thousand parameters each — and the zero-initialized gates make every block start as the identity, which stabilizes training. The DiT ablation found exactly this (adaLN-Zero) best on quality and compute.
