<TLDR>A causal DiT is a transformer that denoises video: pixels are compressed into grids of
16-number cells, the grids are cut into tiles and lined up as a list of 5120-wide tokens, a stack of
shape-preserving blocks denoises that list at every noise level, and generation runs chunk by chunk —
each chunk reading only finished ones — which is what lets a video model close a robot's control
loop.</TLDR>

## Intuition — and a map of this page

Three renames turn a language model into a video diffusion model. Words become **latent patches**
(compressed pieces of video); next-token prediction becomes **denoising** (predict the flow-matching
velocity for every noisy token — see [Flow Matching](../flow-matching/)); and the decoder-only
causal mask becomes **block-causal over chunks**: a chunk being denoised may read every finished
chunk, never the future. Everything else — attention, MLPs, residuals, KV caching — is the same
machinery LLMs use.

The page follows that construction in order. **Part 1** builds the object the model actually reads,
following 1.6 s of real video from pixels to tokens with every shape shown. **Part 2** makes the
video encoder causal in time. **Part 3** opens one DiT block with dimensions on every wire.
**Part 4** chains chunks together with masks and a cache. **Part 5** shows who actually does the
denoising. Every term is defined at first use and collected in the Glossary.

## Part 1 · From pixels to tokens: follow 1.6 seconds of video

A transformer cannot eat an image, a video, or a "16 × 60 × 104" anything. It eats exactly one kind
of object: **a list of same-length vectors** (the tokens). So the honest question — *this shape
becomes what?* — has a one-line answer: it becomes a list of 5120-long vectors. This part shows
each step of that becoming, following one concrete object all the way: **1.6 seconds of one
832 × 480 camera view**.

**Step 1 — space: pixel blocks become cells.** The VAE encoder slides over each frame and replaces
every 8 × 8 block of pixels (8 · 8 · 3 = 192 raw numbers) with **one cell: 16 learned numbers**
that summarize the block's content — edges, color, texture — instead of storing every pixel. A
480 × 832 frame therefore shrinks to a **60 × 104 grid of cells** (480 ÷ 8 and 832 ÷ 8). That is
all the phrase "a small grid of vectors" means, and all the shape notation means:
16 × 60 × 104 = (numbers per cell) × (grid rows) × (grid columns).

**Step 2 — time: frames merge into latent frames.** The same encoder also compresses along time —
each group of 4 consecutive frames merges into **one latent frame** (how it does this without
peeking at the future is Part 2). Our 8 raw frames become **K = 2 latent frames**:

**Step 3 — format: the grid becomes a list.** Attention needs a list of same-length vectors, and a
16 × 60 × 104 slab is not one. So: cut the 60 × 104 grid into **2 × 2 tiles** — the notation
"patch 1 × 2 × 2" just names the tile's size as (time × height × width): 1 latent frame thick,
2 cells tall, 2 cells wide. That yields 30 × 52 = **1,560 tiles per latent frame**. Each tile
contains 4 cells × 16 numbers = **64 numbers**; flatten them into one row and multiply by a single
learned **5120 × 64 matrix** (the *patch embedding*) to get **one token of 5120 numbers**:

## Part 2 · The causal part: no peeking at the future, and an exact anchor

A 3-D convolution is an ordinary convolution whose kernel spans time as well as space,
<M tex="(k_t, k_h, k_w)" />. The catch is *where* the temporal window sits: centered (the default),
the output at time <M tex="\tau" /> reads <M tex="\tau - 1, \tau, \tau + 1" /> — it sees the
future, useless for streaming. Making it causal is one shift, not new machinery:

<Definition term="Causal temporal convolution">
A convolution over time whose output at step <M tex="\tau" /> depends only on inputs at steps
<M tex="\le \tau" />. Implementation: pad <M tex="k_t - 1" /> frames on the *past* side and none on
the future side, so the kernel window ends at the present. Stacking layers grows the receptive
field backward only (the WaveNet construction, applied here to video).
</Definition>

Two consequences of Fig. DIT-3 matter downstream. Strided causal stages do the temporal
downsampling (each stride-2 stage halves the frame count; two stages give the ÷4 used in Step 2
above), and the **anchor** — frame 1's latent, computed from frame 1 alone — is what lets an
image-to-video model condition on a real frame exactly, and lets the encoder run on a *growing*
stream without ever revisiting what it already encoded. The anchor also settles an arithmetic that
otherwise looks off by one: the 8 chunked latent frames of DreamZero's context each summarize 4 raw
frames, and the anchor rides alongside them as the conditioning frame — 1 + 8 × 4 = **33 raw
frames ↔ 8 chunked latent frames** (the paper states the correspondence; the split is the
Wan-family causal-VAE design).

One chunk, then, is three clocks reading the same 1.6 s of wall time — and the 48 actions are
denoised **together, in one pass**, then *streamed* one per 33 ms by the 30 Hz controller while the
GPU already denoises the next chunk. Autoregression lives at chunk granularity only, never
action-by-action:

## Part 3 · One DiT block, with every shape on the wire

Chop, embed, list — now the trunk. A DiT is <M tex="N = 40" /> copies of one block, and one block
is easier than it looks: it is **the same wrapper applied twice** — normalize, re-tune for the noise
level, operate, gate, add the untouched copy back — differing only in the operation wrapped.
Sub-block 1 wraps *attention*, the single place where the <M tex="n" /> tokens exchange information
(its weights are <M tex="n \times n" /> per head); sub-block 2 wraps the *MLP*, which transforms
each token alone. The whole stack is **shape-preserving**: a list of <M tex="n" /> tokens of width
5120 goes in, the same shape comes out, forty times over. Everything else in
the drawing is plumbing for stability (norms, residuals) or for conditioning (the right-hand column):

What makes it a *diffusion* transformer is the right-hand column. The network must act as a whole family
of denoisers — coarse structure-guessing at high noise, fine sharpening at low noise — so the
timestep must be able to re-tune what every block computes:

<Definition term="Adaptive layer norm (adaLN / adaLN-Zero)">
With conditioning vector <M tex="c" /> (here: the timestep embedding), an MLP produces per-channel
modulation signals, and each sub-block computes

<MathBlock>{`\\mathrm{adaLN}(h;\\, c) \\;=\\; \\gamma(c) \\odot \\frac{h - \\mu(h)}{\\sigma(h)} + \\beta(c), \\qquad h' \\;=\\; h + \\alpha(c) \\odot \\mathrm{SubBlock}\\bigl(\\mathrm{adaLN}(h;\\, c)\\bigr)`}</MathBlock>

where SubBlock is self-attention or the MLP. In adaLN-Zero the gates <M tex="\\alpha" /> are
initialized to zero, so every block starts as the identity and training grows the computation
gradually.
</Definition>

### Why scaling-and-shifting, of all things?

Compare the three standard ways to inject a conditioning signal. **As an extra token?**
<M tex="t" /> is *global* — every token in the chunk shares the same value — so attention would
spend capacity re-fetching one constant fact, block after block. **Concatenated once at the
input?** Forty blocks later the signal has washed out, and the late blocks are precisely the ones
whose behavior must differ most across noise levels. **Feature-wise modulation at every block**
(the <Term def="Feature-wise Linear Modulation (Perez et al. 2018): conditioning enters a network as per-channel scales and shifts applied to intermediate activations">FiLM</Term>
pattern, which adaLN instantiates): a per-channel scale can amplify, suppress, or flip a feature —
it *reconfigures* each block's computation — for a few thousand numbers per block, applied
uniformly to all tokens, exactly matching the global nature of the information. The normalization
layer is the natural injection site because LN has just stripped each token's scale and shift;
re-introducing them *as functions of the conditioning* costs nothing structurally. And the
zero-initialized gates mean every residual branch starts closed: each block begins as the identity,
and training opens the gates gradually — which is what makes a 40-block denoiser stable. The DiT
paper ablated the alternatives (in-context token, cross-attention) and adaLN-Zero won on both
quality and compute (Peebles &amp; Xie 2023).

### Why sinusoidal features, and not the raw scalar?

<Definition term="Sinusoidal (Fourier) features">
The scalar <M tex="t" /> is encoded as its readings on a bank of sinusoids at geometrically spaced
frequencies,

<MathBlock>{`e(t) \\;=\\; \\bigl[\\, \\sin(\\omega_1 t),\\ \\cos(\\omega_1 t),\\ \\ldots,\\ \\sin(\\omega_K t),\\ \\cos(\\omega_K t) \\,\\bigr], \\qquad \\omega_{k+1} = r\\,\\omega_k`}</MathBlock>

— the same construction as transformer positional encodings, standard in diffusion models since
DDPM.
</Definition>

Why bother, when <M tex="t" /> is a perfectly good number? Because a plain MLP fed one raw scalar
is biased toward *overly smooth* functions of it (the spectral-bias result — Fourier features are
the standard cure), while a denoiser's behavior genuinely must change quickly in places, most
sharply near the ends of the schedule. The bank fixes this at every scale at once: slow waves
encode early-versus-late, fast waves separate neighboring timesteps, nearby <M tex="t" /> always
map to nearby vectors (no binning cliffs), and the code has constant norm with rich structure the
per-block heads can slice by frequency. One scalar in, a whole spectrum of handles out.

## Part 4 · From clip generator to controller: chunks, masks, cache

A stock video DiT is **bidirectional**: it emits one fixed-length clip, every frame attending to
every other — no notion of "now", so no way to act mid-clip, and fixed windows force frame
subsampling that breaks the video-time ↔ action-time alignment. The causal variant generates
**chunk by chunk** under a mask that is the video version of an LLM's causal mask:

<Definition term="Block-causal attention mask">
Group the sequence into chunks <M tex="1, \dots, M" />. A query token in chunk <M tex="i" /> may
attend to a key token in chunk <M tex="j" /> iff <M tex="j < i" /> (finished, clean context) or
<M tex="j = i" /> (its own window, bidirectional). Conditioning tokens (text, proprioception) are
attendable from every chunk.
</Definition>

Finished chunks persist as a
<Term def="cached key/value tensors of already-processed tokens; the model re-reads its whole past for the price of a lookup instead of recomputation">KV cache</Term>,
so context grows almost for free (up to 4 chunks = 6.6 s here). Explore the masks — and the one
move unique to a *robot* video model:

<AttnMask />

Training uses
<Term def="conditioning on the ground-truth past rather than the model's own predictions; fast and stable, but mismatched with inference, where the model must consume its own outputs">teacher forcing</Term>:
previous chunks in context are clean ground truth. At inference a pure video generator must feed on
its own imperfect outputs, and the mismatch compounds — autoregressive **drift**. A closed-loop
robot model has an exit no offline generator has: after each chunk *executes*, the real camera
frames overwrite the model's predicted frames in the KV cache, restoring teacher-forcing conditions
every 1.6 s (toggle it in Fig. DIT-2's inference tab). Offline cousins — noise injection on
context, diffusion forcing — only soften the mismatch; observation overwrite deletes it, at the
price of needing an environment that answers back.

And the tokens come back out the way they went in, reversed: after the loop exits at
<M tex="t = 1" />, a final linear layer maps each video token 5120 → 64, those 64 numbers fold back
into the token's 2 × 2 tile (**un-patchify** — Fig. DIT-8 run right-to-left), reassembling the
16 × 60 × 104 latent frames for the VAE decoder, while the action tokens pass through the action
decoder into the 48 executable joint deltas.

## Glossary

- <Term def="a fixed-width vector in the transformer's working space; every input (latent patch, word piece, action slice, state reading) becomes one before entering the blocks">token</Term>
- <Term def="the compressed representation a VAE encoder produces from pixels; all generation happens on these small grids, pixels only appear at the ends">latent</Term>
- <Term def="variational autoencoder: encoder compressing frames into latents + decoder reconstructing pixels; trained once, then frozen and reused">VAE</Term>
- <Term def="a small tile of the latent grid; one patch becomes one token">patch</Term>
- <Term def="each token reads a weighted mix of all (unmasked) tokens; the weights are computed from the tokens themselves">self-attention</Term>
- <Term def="adaptive layer norm: the timestep (and other conditioning) is turned into per-block scale and shift applied at the normalization layers — how one network denoises at every noise level (Peebles & Xie 2023)">adaLN</Term>
- <Term def="a learned vector encoding the scalar denoising time t, fed to every block via adaLN">timestep embedding</Term>
- <Term def="the block's input added back to its output; keeps deep stacks trainable">residual</Term>
- <Term def="cached key/value tensors of already-processed tokens; the model re-reads its whole past for the price of a lookup instead of recomputation">KV cache</Term>
- <Term def="attention rule at chunk granularity: full attention within a chunk, causal (past-only) across chunks">block-causal mask</Term>
- <Term def="the generation unit: a short span of latent frames and their aligned actions denoised together (DreamZero: 2 latent frames + 48 actions = 1.6 s)">chunk</Term>
- <Term def="training on ground-truth past context rather than the model's own predictions; stable, but mismatched with inference">teacher forcing</Term>
- <Term def="compounding error when an autoregressive model conditions on its own imperfect outputs; small mistakes feed back and grow">drift</Term>
- <Term def="after a chunk executes, real camera frames replace the model's predicted frames in the KV cache — restoring teacher-forcing conditions at inference; available only to closed-loop (robot) models">observation overwrite</Term>
- <Term def="a model generating one fixed-length clip with every frame attending to every other; no notion of 'now', so unusable for control">bidirectional video model</Term>
- <Term def="a video VAE built from 3-D convolutions that are causal along time (no future peeking), with the first frame encoded exactly; enables streaming encode/decode and clean image conditioning">causal video VAE</Term>
- <Term def="cutting a latent grid into small tiles — named (time × height × width), e.g. 1 × 2 × 2 = one frame thick, two cells tall, two cells wide — and projecting each tile's numbers to the model width; one tile becomes one token">patchify</Term>
- <Term def="the adaLN variant whose gates are initialized to zero, so every block starts as the identity and training grows the computation gradually">adaLN-Zero</Term>
- <Term def="a fixed-horizon block of H consecutive actions predicted in one shot and executed sequentially by the controller; here H = 48 at 30 Hz">action chunk</Term>
- <Term def="the set of input positions that can influence a given output; causal stacking grows it into the past only">receptive field</Term>
- <Term def="a convolution that steps by more than one position, emitting fewer outputs — the standard downsampling move (stride 2 in time halves the frame count)">strided convolution</Term>
- <Term def="Feature-wise Linear Modulation: conditioning enters as per-channel scales and shifts on intermediate activations; adaLN is FiLM applied at the normalization layers">FiLM</Term>
- <Term def="encoding a scalar by its readings on sines/cosines at geometrically spaced frequencies; gives smooth, multi-scale, constant-norm codes that MLPs can use at high frequency">sinusoidal (Fourier) features</Term>

<SelfCheck q="Why does chunk-causal generation matter for a robot, beyond latency?" a="Alignment and statefulness. Bidirectional models need fixed-length windows, which forces video subsampling that distorts the correspondence between frame time and action time; causal chunks preserve native rates. And the KV cache is a visual memory: each new chunk conditions on up to 6.6 s of actual history — at inference, real observed history, thanks to the overwrite." />
<SelfCheck q="Where exactly does the LLM analogy break?" a="Two places. Tokens within a chunk are denoised jointly with bidirectional attention among themselves (an LLM is causal token-by-token), and the model predicts continuous velocities for latent patches rather than a softmax over a vocabulary. The causal structure lives at chunk granularity, not token granularity." />
<SelfCheck q="Why do 8 latent frames correspond to 33 raw frames rather than 32?" a="Because the VAE is causal with an exact anchor: the first raw frame is encoded alone as the conditioning frame, and each of the 8 chunked latent frames summarizes 4 raw frames — 1 + 8 × 4 = 33, with the anchor riding alongside the 8 rather than being one of them. The anchor is what makes image-to-video conditioning exact and streaming encoding possible; a plain non-causal 4× VAE would give the rounder 32." />
<SelfCheck q="Why is t injected at all 40 blocks via scale-and-shift, rather than fed once as an input or a token?" a="Because t must reconfigure the whole family of computations: early blocks and late blocks both change behavior with noise level, and a signal injected once at the bottom washes out by the top. As a token it would waste attention on a value every token already shares. Per-block FiLM-style scales and shifts can amplify, suppress or flip each feature channel — reconfiguring every block for a few thousand parameters each — and the zero-initialized gates make every block start as the identity, which stabilizes training. The DiT ablation found exactly this (adaLN-Zero) best on quality and compute." />
