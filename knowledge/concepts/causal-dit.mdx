<TLDR>A DiT is a standard transformer whose tokens are noisy latent patches and whose output is a
denoising target; making it causal — generating in chunks that attend only to finished chunks via a
KV cache — is what turns a video generator into something that can run a control loop.</TLDR>

## Intuition

Three renames turn a language model into a video diffusion model. Words become **latent patches**
(compressed image pieces); next-token prediction becomes **denoising** (predict the flow-matching
velocity for every noisy token — see [Flow Matching](../flow-matching/)); and the decoder-only
causal mask becomes **block-causal over chunks**: a chunk of frames being denoised may read every
finished chunk, never the future. Everything else — attention, MLPs, residuals, KV caching — is the
same machinery LLMs use.

## Latents first: why nothing here touches pixels

A 480P frame is ~400k pixels, mostly texture that carries no scene information. Video models
therefore run every frame through a
<Term def="variational autoencoder: an encoder compressing frames into a small latent grid plus a decoder reconstructing pixels; trained once, then frozen and reused">VAE</Term>
and do all generation on the compressed **latents**; pixels appear only at the ends. The compression
is spatial and temporal — in DreamZero's Wan2.1 backbone, 8 latent frames stand in for 33 raw frames
(6.6 s at 5 FPS). When a WAM paper says "video", read "a small grid of latent vectors".

How does the compression actually work? The encoder is a **3-D convolutional network**: its kernels
slide over time, height and width *together*, so one latent frame summarizes a short span of raw
frames, not a single one — and the convolutions are
<Term def="a convolution whose temporal receptive field only reaches backward: an output at time t depends on inputs at or before t, never after">causal along time</Term>,
so no latent frame ever peeks at the future (which is what lets the same VAE encode a growing video
stream chunk by chunk). The first frame is encoded exactly, on its own — the *anchor* — which makes
image-to-video conditioning clean; every later latent step summarizes about 4 raw frames. That is
where the odd-looking correspondence comes from: 1 + 4 × 8 = 33 raw frames ↔ 8 latent frames.

## The DiT block

Chop the latent grid into patches, project each to a fixed-width vector (a **token**), and stack the
standard loop: attention (every token reads a weighted mix of the others), MLP, residuals. Two
things make it a *diffusion* transformer: tokens are noisy latents, and the timestep <M tex="t" />
enters **every block** by scaling and shifting the normalization layers (the adaLN recipe of Peebles
& Xie 2023) — one network thereby serves every noise level:

same pattern around an MLP. Bottom lane: the timestep pathway — the scalar t becomes sinusoidal
features, an MLP turns them into a conditioning vector c, and a per-block head emits the six
modulation signals (two scale-shift pairs, two gates) injected at the accent points. One network
thereby serves every noise level. DreamZero inherits Wan2.1's blocks; this drawing is the standard

DreamZero paper.</figcaption>

Two readings of the drawing worth making explicit. First, plain <Term def="layer norm: normalize each token's features to zero mean and unit variance, then apply a scale and a shift">LN</Term>
uses a *fixed, learned* scale and shift; the adaptive version replaces those constants with
*functions of the conditioning* — that is the entire trick:

<Definition term="Adaptive layer norm (adaLN / adaLN-Zero)">
With conditioning vector <M tex="c" /> (here: the timestep embedding), an MLP produces per-channel
modulation signals, and each sub-block computes

<MathBlock>{`\\mathrm{adaLN}(h;\\, c) \\;=\\; \\gamma(c) \\odot \\frac{h - \\mu(h)}{\\sigma(h)} + \\beta(c), \\qquad h' \\;=\\; h + \\alpha(c) \\odot \\mathrm{SubBlock}\\bigl(\\mathrm{adaLN}(h;\\, c)\\bigr)`}</MathBlock>

where SubBlock is self-attention or the MLP. In adaLN-Zero the gates <M tex="\\alpha" /> are
initialized to zero, so every block starts as the identity and training grows the computation
gradually.
</Definition>

Second, the timestep's full route: the scalar <M tex="t" /> is encoded as sinusoidal features
(smooth sin/cos at many frequencies — the same trick as positional encodings, so nearby noise
levels get nearby codes), an MLP maps those to one conditioning vector <M tex="c" />, and each
block's small head maps <M tex="c" /> to its own six signals <M tex="(\\gamma_1, \\beta_1, \\alpha_1, \\gamma_2, \\beta_2, \\alpha_2)" />.
The timestep is never a token in this recipe: it acts multiplicatively and additively on every
block's activations, which is how one set of weights can behave like a family of denoisers — one
per noise level.

Attention over **one shared sequence** is also what makes multimodal joint modeling real rather than
a slogan: video tokens, action tokens, text tokens and state tokens can sit in the same sequence, so
(in a WAM) action tokens read the imagined future directly at every block. Nothing in the trunk is
modality- or robot-specific — which is precisely why a video-pretrained trunk transfers.

## Making it causal: chunks, KV cache, teacher forcing

A stock video DiT is **bidirectional**: it emits one fixed-length clip, every frame attending to
every other. Two properties disqualify that for control: you need actions *now*, not after the clip;
and fixed windows force frame subsampling that breaks alignment between video time and action time.
The causal variant generates **chunk by chunk** — in DreamZero, a chunk is <M tex="K=2" /> latent
frames paired with <M tex="H=48" /> actions, 1.6 s of world — under a **block-causal mask**: the
chunk being denoised attends to all *finished* chunks and to itself, never the future. Finished
chunks persist as a
<Term def="cached key/value tensors of already-processed tokens; an autoregressive model re-reads its whole past for the price of a lookup instead of recomputation">KV cache</Term>,
so context grows almost for free.

<Definition term="Block-causal attention mask">
Group the sequence into chunks <M tex="1, \dots, M" />. A query token in chunk <M tex="i" /> may
attend to a key token in chunk <M tex="j" /> iff <M tex="j < i" /> (finished, clean context) or
<M tex="j = i" /> (its own window, bidirectional). Conditioning tokens (text, proprioception) are
attendable from every chunk.
</Definition>
 What does "K = 2 latent frames paired with H = 48 actions, 1.6 s of world" mean concretely? Three
clocks describing the same physical time span — and yes: the 48 actions are denoised **at once**, as
one action chunk, then *streamed* sequentially by the 30 Hz controller over the next 1.6 s while the
GPU denoises the following chunk. The numbers lock together: 1.6 s × 5 FPS = 8 raw frames; 8 raw
frames ÷ 4 (temporal VAE compression) = 2 latent frames; 1.6 s × 30 Hz = 48 actions. Autoregression
lives at chunk granularity only — never action-by-action.

Training uses
<Term def="conditioning on the ground-truth past rather than the model's own predictions; fast and stable, but mismatched with inference, where the model must consume its own outputs">teacher forcing</Term>:
previous chunks in context are clean ground truth. At inference a pure video generator must feed on
its own imperfect outputs, and the mismatch compounds — autoregressive **drift**. A closed-loop
robot model has an exit no offline generator has: after each chunk *executes*, the real camera
frames can overwrite the predicted frames in the KV cache, restoring teacher-forcing conditions
every chunk (DreamZero, §3.1 — toggle it in Fig. DIT-2's inference tab). Cousin fixes in the
offline world — noise injection on context, diffusion forcing — only soften the mismatch;
observation overwrite deletes it, at the price of needing an environment that answers back.

## Glossary

- <Term def="a fixed-width vector in the transformer's working space; every input (latent patch, word piece, action slice, state reading) becomes one before entering the blocks">token</Term>
- <Term def="the compressed representation a VAE encoder produces from pixels; all generation happens on these small grids, pixels only appear at the ends">latent</Term>
- <Term def="variational autoencoder: encoder compressing frames into latents + decoder reconstructing pixels; trained once, then frozen and reused">VAE</Term>
- <Term def="a small tile of the latent grid; one patch becomes one token">patch</Term>
- <Term def="each token reads a weighted mix of all (unmasked) tokens; the weights are computed from the tokens themselves">self-attention</Term>
- <Term def="adaptive layer norm: the timestep (and other conditioning) is turned into per-block scale and shift applied at the normalization layers — how one network denoises at every noise level (Peebles & Xie 2023)">adaLN</Term>
- <Term def="a learned vector encoding the scalar denoising time t, fed to every block via adaLN">timestep embedding</Term>
- <Term def="the block's input added back to its output; keeps deep stacks trainable">residual</Term>
- <Term def="cached key/value tensors of already-processed tokens; the model re-reads its whole past for the price of a lookup instead of recomputation">KV cache</Term>
- <Term def="attention rule at chunk granularity: full attention within a chunk, causal (past-only) across chunks">block-causal mask</Term>
- <Term def="the generation unit: a short span of latent frames and their aligned actions denoised together (DreamZero: 2 latent frames + 48 actions = 1.6 s)">chunk</Term>
- <Term def="training on ground-truth past context rather than the model's own predictions; stable, but mismatched with inference">teacher forcing</Term>
- <Term def="compounding error when an autoregressive model conditions on its own imperfect outputs; small mistakes feed back and grow">drift</Term>
- <Term def="after a chunk executes, real camera frames replace the model's predicted frames in the KV cache — restoring teacher-forcing conditions at inference; available only to closed-loop (robot) models">observation overwrite</Term>
- <Term def="a model generating one fixed-length clip with every frame attending to every other; no notion of 'now', so unusable for control">bidirectional video model</Term>
- <Term def="a video VAE built from 3-D convolutions that are causal along time (no future peeking), with the first frame encoded exactly; enables streaming encode/decode and clean image conditioning">causal video VAE</Term>
- <Term def="cutting a latent grid into small tiles and projecting each tile to the model width — one tile becomes one token">patchify</Term>
- <Term def="the adaLN variant whose gates are initialized to zero, so every block starts as the identity and training grows the computation gradually">adaLN-Zero</Term>
- <Term def="a fixed-horizon block of H consecutive actions predicted in one shot and executed sequentially by the controller; here H = 48 at 30 Hz">action chunk</Term>

<SelfCheck q="Why does chunk-causal generation matter for a robot, beyond latency?" a="Alignment and statefulness. Bidirectional models need fixed-length windows, which forces video subsampling that distorts the correspondence between frame time and action time; causal chunks preserve native rates. And the KV cache is a visual memory: each new chunk conditions on up to 6.6 s of actual history — at inference, real observed history, thanks to the overwrite." />
<SelfCheck q="Where exactly does the LLM analogy break?" a="Two places. Tokens within a chunk are denoised jointly with bidirectional attention among themselves (an LLM is causal token-by-token), and the model predicts continuous velocities for latent patches rather than a softmax over a vocabulary. The causal structure lives at chunk granularity, not token granularity." />
<SelfCheck q="Why do 8 latent frames correspond to 33 raw frames rather than 32?" a="Because the VAE is causal with an exact anchor: the first raw frame is encoded alone, and each subsequent latent step summarizes about 4 raw frames — 1 + 4 × 8 = 33. The anchor is what makes image-to-video conditioning exact and streaming encoding possible; a non-causal 4× VAE would give the rounder 32." />
