<TLDR>Flow matching trains a network to predict the velocity of a straight-line journey from noise to
data; generating a sample is then just integrating that velocity field. It is the objective behind
today's large video/image models (SD3, Flux, Wan) — and behind DreamZero's joint video+action
denoising.</TLDR>

## Intuition

A generative model must answer: *given pure randomness, produce something that looks like the data.*
Flow matching's answer is transport. Imagine every possible data sample connected to a random noise
sample by a straight line. If a network could tell you, at any intermediate point, "which way is
home and how fast", you could start anywhere in the noise and simply walk to a data sample. Training
teaches exactly that one **job**: look at a partially noisy point and the time <M tex="t" />, output
the direction-and-speed of the line passing through it. (The word is "job", not "skill" — in
robotics papers *skill* means a motion primitive; here nothing robotic is meant.)

## First, the vocabulary: field, probability path, marginal

Three words do the heavy lifting in every flow-matching paper, and none of them is exotic.

A <Term def="a function that assigns a value to every point of a space; a number per point is a scalar field, an arrow per point is a vector field">field</Term>
assigns a value to *every point of space*. A temperature map is a scalar field — one number per
location. A wind map is a **vector field** — one arrow per location. That is all "field" means here:
not a trajectory, not a path, but a *function* you can query anywhere. (And not the algebra
sense: the "field" of group–ring–field is a number system closed under the four operations —
German distinguishes *Feld*, this one, from *Körper*, that one; English collapsed both. The only
contact point: velocity vectors live in a vector space, which is defined *over* the algebraic field
ℝ of scalars.) A
<Term def="a vector field that may also change over time: for every location x and time t it returns one arrow (direction and speed), u(x, t)">velocity field</Term>
<M tex="u(x, t)" /> is a wind map that may change over time: feed it any location <M tex="x" /> and
any time <M tex="t" />, it returns the one arrow at that place and moment.

A <Term def="a continuously indexed family of distributions p_t interpolating from the noise distribution p_0 to the data distribution p_1 — a movie whose frames are distributions">probability path</Term>
<M tex="p_t" /> is a *movie of distributions*: frame 0 is the noise distribution, frame 1 is the
data distribution, and the frames in between morph one into the other continuously. The
straight-line interpolant picks one specific movie — at time <M tex="t" />, the crowd of points is
exactly the pairwise blends <M tex="t\,x_1 + (1-t)\,x_0" />.

The two notions meet in one verb: a velocity field **generates** a probability path when blowing the
crowd with that wind reproduces the movie — scatter points as frame 0, move each one by
<M tex="\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}t = u(x, t)" />, and at every <M tex="t" /> the crowd is distributed
exactly as frame <M tex="t" />. Flow matching's whole object of desire is one such field.

## The exact definition

Fix two distributions in the same space: noise <M tex="p_0 = \mathcal{N}(0, I)" /> and the data
distribution <M tex="p_1" />. Choose a **probability path** <M tex="p_t" /> that morphs one into the
other as <M tex="t" /> runs 0 → 1. A time-dependent **velocity field** <M tex="u(x, t)" />
*generates* that path if moving every point by the ODE <M tex="\mathrm{d}x/\mathrm{d}t = u(x, t)" />
carries <M tex="p_0" /> into exactly <M tex="p_t" /> at every time. Flow matching = fit a network
<M tex="u_\theta" /> to such a field by plain regression.

The marginal field is intractable, so **conditional flow matching** (the trick that makes it work)
regresses against the velocity of the path *conditioned on a sampled pair*: draw a data point
<M tex="x_1" />, a noise point <M tex="x_0" />, a time <M tex="t" />, form the interpolant, and
regress on that pair's own velocity. The theorem making this legal (Lipman et al. 2022): the
conditional objective has the **same gradients** as the intractable marginal one, so its minimizer
is the true marginal field. With the straight-line (rectified-flow) path used in practice:

<MathBlock>{`x_t = t\\,x_1 + (1-t)\\,x_0, \\qquad \\mathcal{L} = \\mathbb{E}_{x_0, x_1, t}\\, \\bigl\\| u_\\theta(x_t, t) - (x_1 - x_0) \\bigr\\|^2`}</MathBlock>

Convention warning: this page (and DreamZero's Eq. 2) puts noise at <M tex="t = 0" /> and data at
<M tex="t = 1" />; many diffusion papers run the axis the other way. Always check before comparing
equations across papers.

### What u_θ actually converges to: the marginal field

Read the loss closely and a puzzle appears — *for a fixed pair the target <M tex="x_1 - x_0" /> is
one constant vector, so is the network supposed to output that same vector at every
<M tex="t" />?* No, and the reason is the heart of the method. The network is a function of
<M tex="(x, t)" /> **only**; it is never told which pair produced its input. The same point
<M tex="x" /> at time <M tex="t" /> can be reached by many different (noise, data) pairs, each
demanding a *different* velocity. A mean-squared-error regression fed conflicting targets converges
to their average — precisely, the conditional expectation

<MathBlock>{`u^{\\star}(x, t) \\;=\\; \\mathbb{E}\\bigl[\\, x_1 - x_0 \\;\\big|\\; x_t = x \\,\\bigr]`}</MathBlock>

This average-over-all-pairings is the
<Term def="the velocity field obtained by averaging every conditional (per-pair) velocity passing through (x, t), weighted by how likely each pairing is; the MSE-optimal network output">marginal velocity field</Term>.
It is *intractable to write down* — evaluating that expectation means integrating over every pairing
consistent with landing at <M tex="x" /> at time <M tex="t" />, weighted by the very data
distribution you are trying to learn. But you never need to write it down: SGD on the per-pair
(**conditional**) loss converges to it automatically, and the flow-matching theorem guarantees that
integrating this *mean* field still transports <M tex="p_0" /> exactly to <M tex="p_1" />.

So the honest description of <M tex="u_\theta(x_t, t)" />: **input** — a position in sample space
and a time; **output** — one velocity vector, the posterior-mean of <M tex="x_1 - x_0" /> over all
pairings that could have put a point there. Near <M tex="t = 0" /> the input is almost pure noise
and says little about the destination, so the posterior is broad and the arrow points toward a blur
of plausible data. Near <M tex="t = 1" /> the point has nearly arrived, the posterior collapses,
and the arrow points at one specific sample. Constant along each *conditional* line, yes — but what
is *learned* is a genuine function of position and time.

### "Rectified flow", named

<Term def="the linear-interpolant instance of flow matching (Liu et al. 2022): straight-line paths x_t = t x1 + (1−t) x0 with velocity target x1 − x0, plus the reflow iterate">Rectified flow</Term>
is the name of exactly this linear-interpolant instance — straight conditional paths, constant
per-pair velocity — introduced by Liu et al. (2022) alongside an optional iterate called
<Term def="re-pair each generated sample with the noise that produced it (a non-crossing coupling) and retrain; each round straightens the marginal trajectories, approaching one-step generation">reflow</Term>:
sample with the trained field, re-pair each output with the noise seed that produced it, retrain on
those non-crossing pairs. Each round straightens the *marginal* trajectories (Fig. FM-2's curving
diminishes), which is the principled route to one-step generation. "Flow matching with the linear
path", "rectified flow", and "stochastic interpolants with linear interpolant" name the same object
from three simultaneous 2022 papers.

## What "velocity" means here — and what it does not

The velocity is the time-derivative of a point's position along the chosen path,
<M tex="\mathrm{d}x_t/\mathrm{d}t" /> — "which direction and how fast this point should move
through the space at time <M tex="t" />". The space is the model's sample space (image latents,
video latents, normalized action chunks). It is **not** a physical velocity: in DreamZero the
flow-matching velocity for action tokens is a derivative in normalized-action space along the
denoising path — it is not the robot's joint velocity, and the executed actions are joint-position
deltas, not speeds.

**Why is it constant along a straight line?** Differentiate the interpolant once:

<MathBlock>{`\\frac{\\mathrm{d}x_t}{\\mathrm{d}t} = \\frac{\\mathrm{d}}{\\mathrm{d}t}\\bigl[\\, t\\,x_1 + (1-t)\\,x_0 \\,\\bigr] = x_1 - x_0`}</MathBlock>

The result contains no <M tex="t" />: position depends on <M tex="t" /> linearly, so its derivative
is the same everywhere on the segment. That constancy is the pedagogical gift of the straight path —
the regression target for a given pair does not change as the point travels, which makes the
learning problem as clean as regression gets.

## Why predict the velocity (and not the clean sample, or the noise)?

Given <M tex="x_t" /> and <M tex="t" />, the three classic targets are affinely interchangeable —
predict any one and the others follow:

<MathBlock>{`x_1 = x_t + (1-t)\\,v, \\qquad x_0 = x_t - t\\,v, \\qquad v = x_1 - x_0`}</MathBlock>

So velocity-prediction is a *parameterization choice*, not extra power. It is the preferred one
because (1) the sampler integrates a velocity field, so the network outputs exactly what the ODE
solver consumes; (2) its scale is well-behaved across all <M tex="t" /> — noise-prediction targets
become ill-conditioned near the data end and clean-sample-prediction becomes trivial there, while
the constant <M tex="x_1 - x_0" /> keeps the loss informative everywhere; and (3) one network
evaluated at any <M tex="(x, t)" /> defines the entire transport at once — a single amortized field
rather than a chain of step-specific denoisers.

## History in five steps

1. **Continuous normalizing flows** (Chen et al. 2018, FFJORD 2019): generate by integrating a
   learned ODE — elegant, but training required simulating the ODE. Too slow to scale.
2. **Score-based diffusion / DDPM** (Song & Ermon 2019; Ho et al. 2020): corrupt data with a fixed
   *stochastic* noising process and learn to reverse it step by step. Simulation-free training —
   this is what scaled first — but sampling wanted many steps along a curved path.
3. **Probability-flow ODE** (Song et al. 2021): every diffusion SDE has a deterministic ODE with the
   same marginals — the bridge showing diffusion and flows are two views of one thing.
4. **Flow matching / rectified flow / stochastic interpolants** (Lipman et al.; Liu et al.; Albergo
   & Vanden-Eijnden — all 2022, independently): train the ODE directly, simulation-free, with freely
   chosen paths — including straight ones.
5. **Adoption** (2024–): Stable Diffusion 3, Flux, Movie Gen, and Wan2.1 — DreamZero's backbone —
   all train with flow-matching objectives.

## Benefits — and the honest drawbacks

Benefits: a stable MSE loss (no adversary, no likelihood machinery); simulation-free training of a
continuous flow; near-straight paths that tolerate few, large integration steps (DreamZero: 16
denoising steps, cut to ~4 by caching directionally-consistent velocities, and to 1 video step in
its Flash variant); and one field usable at every noise level.

Drawbacks of replacing the curved stochastic path with a straight deterministic one:

- **Individually straight, collectively curved.** Conditional paths cross (visible in Fig. FM-1 —
  lines intersect), and the learned *marginal* field is an average over crossings (Fig. FM-2), so actual sampled
  trajectories still bend. True one-step generation needs extra work (reflow, distillation).
- **No stochastic self-correction.** An SDE sampler re-injects noise each step, which can act as a
  corrector that "forgets" small integration errors; a deterministic ODE accumulates them. Some
  quality regimes still favor stochastic samplers.
- **Determinism means diversity comes only from the seed.** Same noise in, same sample out — fine in
  practice, but worth knowing.
- **It is largely diffusion, reparameterized.** Under the hood, straight-path flow matching is
  equivalent to a specific weighting/parameterization of denoising objectives (the Kingma & Gao
  view), so the schedule and loss weight <M tex="w(t)" /> still matter — the gains are practical
  (stability, step count), not a new capability class.

## In this library

DreamZero applies exactly this objective jointly to video latents and action latents, with one
shared <M tex="t" /> per chunk so both modalities denoise toward the same imagined future — and its
Flash variant deliberately *breaks* the shared schedule (video <M tex="t \sim \mathrm{Beta}(7,1)" />,

## Glossary

- <Term def="a function assigning a value to every point of a space — a number per point (scalar field, e.g. a temperature map) or an arrow per point (vector field, e.g. a wind map); queryable anywhere, not a trajectory. Unrelated to the algebraic field of group/ring/field: that is a number system closed under + − × ÷ (ℝ, ℚ, ℂ). German keeps them distinct — Feld (this sense) vs Körper (the algebraic one) — English merged both into one word">field</Term>
- <Term def="a time-dependent vector field u(x, t): one arrow (direction + speed) for every location and moment">velocity field</Term>
- <Term def="a continuously indexed family of distributions p_t morphing noise p_0 into data p_1 — a movie whose frames are distributions">probability path</Term>
- <Term def="a velocity field generates a path when moving points by dx/dt = u(x,t) makes the crowd match p_t at every t">generates</Term>
- <Term def="the per-pair straight-line target x_1 − x_0: the velocity of the path conditioned on one sampled (noise, data) pair">conditional velocity</Term>
- <Term def="the average of all conditional velocities passing through (x, t), weighted by pairing likelihood: u*(x,t) = E[x_1 − x_0 | x_t = x]; the MSE-optimal network and the field the sampler integrates">marginal velocity field</Term>
- <Term def="the average of a random quantity restricted to cases consistent with an observation — here, average target velocity given that the point sits at x at time t">conditional expectation</Term>
- <Term def="ordinary differential equation dx/dt = u(x,t): deterministic motion prescribed by a velocity field; solving it numerically = integration (e.g. Euler steps)">ODE</Term>
- <Term def="stochastic differential equation: an ODE plus injected randomness at every step — the mathematical home of classic diffusion sampling">SDE</Term>
- <Term def="the deterministic ODE that shares every marginal distribution with a given diffusion SDE (Song et al. 2021); the bridge between diffusion and flows">probability-flow ODE</Term>
- <Term def="training without ever solving the ODE/SDE during training — the property that let diffusion and flow matching scale where classic CNFs could not">simulation-free</Term>
- <Term def="any x_t built by blending a data sample with a noise sample, e.g. the linear x_t = t·x_1 + (1−t)·x_0; 'stochastic interpolants' is the general framework">interpolant</Term>
- <Term def="the linear-interpolant instance of flow matching (Liu et al. 2022), straight conditional paths + constant per-pair velocity">rectified flow</Term>
- <Term def="re-pair generated samples with their own noise seeds and retrain; straightens marginal trajectories toward one-step generation">reflow</Term>
- <Term def="conditional flow matching: the tractable objective regressing on per-pair velocities, whose gradients equal the intractable marginal objective's">CFM</Term>
- <Term def="classifier-free guidance: a conditional and an unconditional forward pass extrapolated against each other to strengthen conditioning">CFG</Term>
