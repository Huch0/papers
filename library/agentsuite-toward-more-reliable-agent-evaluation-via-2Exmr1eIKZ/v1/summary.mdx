<TLDR>AgentSuite is a unified runner for LLM tool-use/agent benchmarks that ships cleaned and filtered variants of eight-plus benchmark suites, with defective tasks identified by COBA — a taxonomy-grounded, two-stage (benchmark-aware rules, then LLM-as-Judge) auditing pipeline with human verification.</TLDR>

<WhyItMatters>Benchmark auditing is squarely in my evaluation-methodology lane: it audits exactly the harness benchmarks I track (tau-bench, tau2-bench, BFCL v3/v4, ACEBench) and quantifies how much reported agent scores ride on defective tasks.</WhyItMatters>

## Motivation
Per the repo's framing, <Problem>agent benchmarks contain identifiable task-level defects — e.g. "malformed tools, incorrect ground truth" (README) — spanning "benchmark tasks, tools, evaluation, and environments" (pipeline/README.md)</Problem>, so <Problem>reported agent scores conflate model failures with benchmark defects, undermining evaluation reliability and cross-benchmark comparability</Problem>. The paper's own statement of the gap versus prior benchmark-quality work is unavailable (see caveat above).

## Contribution (what's new)
Two deliverables, per the repo:
- <Novelty>COBA (Component-Based Benchmark Auditing): a pipeline that "operationalizes a structured taxonomy of benchmark task-level issues and applies a two-stage process combining rule-based detection and LLM-based assessment, with human verification"</Novelty> (src: pipeline/README.md). Stage 1 applies benchmark-aware deterministic rules for "structural or misalignment issues"; Stage 2 uses <Term def="using an LLM to grade or flag items instead of a deterministic checker">LLM-as-Judge</Term> for "more subtle or semantic issues that are difficult to capture via rules".
- <Novelty>AgentSuite: one-touch execution of the audited benchmarks plus cleaned benchmark variants that "mitigate identifiable evaluation issues while preserving comparability with original benchmark settings"</Novelty> (src: README), surfaced as Original-vs-Filtering scores on a public Hugging Face leaderboard.

## Methodology
As far as the repo documents it — the paper's method section is unavailable.

**What the audit consumes.** Recorded evaluation trajectories, not live runs: per-model JSONL files containing the full message/tool-call trace plus an eval result; "This pipeline does NOT run benchmark evaluations" (src: pipeline/README.md). The default Stage-2 judge is google/gemini-2.5-pro-thinking-on, with an optional rebuttal round (`--rebuttal`).

**What it outputs.** Per benchmark: identified issue cases per task, rule-based and LLM-based filtering decisions, "alignment with human-labeled ground truth" (precision/recall against a `human_labelled_ground_truth/` set), and retention statistics (src: pipeline/README.md).

**Benchmarks audited.** ACEBench, <Term def="Berkeley Function-Calling Leaderboard (the gorilla project); v3 adds multi-turn, v4 agentic tasks">BFCL</Term> v3 and v4, ComplexFuncBench, <Term def="tool-agent-user benchmarks simulating retail/airline (tau2 adds telecom) customer-service agents with a simulated user">tau-bench and tau2-bench</Term>, DrafterBench, NexusBench (src: pipeline/README.md); the runner additionally wraps MultiChallenge, for 11 runnable configs including separate BFCL prompting and function-calling modes (src: README). The corresponding harnesses (gorilla, tau-bench, tau2-bench, ACEBench, ...) are vendored in the repo tree.

**Cleaned variants.** Flagged tasks live in per-benchmark `<bench>_issues.csv` manifests feeding two variants: **Filtered** drops flagged tasks and re-scores existing results with no reruns (the launch variant); **Fixed** applies targeted repairs on fixed data and drops unfixable tasks (src: README). <Finding src="README">Repo-documented filtered task counts: ACEBench 1023 to 999, ComplexFuncBench 1000 to 871, tau-bench airline 50 to 39 and retail 115 to 109, tau2-bench airline 50 to 31, retail 114 to 102, telecom 114 to 104</Finding> — flag rates from roughly 2% of a suite up to 38% of a split. <Finding src="README">DrafterBench issues found by the audit were reported upstream and fixed there</Finding>, so no additional filtering is applied to it.
