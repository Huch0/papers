<TLDR>Llama 2 pairs a stronger open base model (2T tokens, 4k context, GQA, commercial license) with the first fully documented industrial RLHF pipeline — quality-first SFT, two reward models, five iterations of rejection sampling + PPO, and Ghost Attention — yielding a chat model that human raters judge on par with ChatGPT on helpfulness and safety.</TLDR>

<WhyItMatters>This is the paper that made open-weight *aligned* models real: before it, open bases existed but the product-grade RLHF recipe was a trade secret. Every open post-training pipeline since (and most of what I know about reward modeling in practice) descends from what this paper chose to write down.</WhyItMatters>

## Motivation

Open pretrained models had caught up; open *assistants* had not. <Problem>BLOOM, LLaMA-1, and Falcon match GPT-3-class base models, but none is a substitute for the closed "product" LLMs — ChatGPT, Bard, Claude — because those are heavily fine-tuned to human preferences</Problem>, and <Problem>that alignment step is costly in compute and annotation and is neither transparent nor reproducible, which blocks the community from doing alignment research at all</Problem> (§1). Distillation-style imitations (Alpaca, Vicuna) narrow the gap superficially but fall short of the closed bar (§6). The missing artifact was not weights — it was the recipe.

## Contribution (what's new)

The core contribution is <Novelty>the recipe itself, published end-to-end with its ablations: iterative RLHF where preference data, reward models, and policy improve together in weekly cycles, so the reward model stays on-distribution for the policy it must judge</Novelty>. The form is forced: a static reward model goes stale as the policy improves (its accuracy degrades off-distribution), so annotation must be interleaved with training, not collected once. Around this core sit deliberate design choices, each justified by a failure it prevents: <Novelty>two separate reward models (helpfulness, safety), because the two objectives trade off and confuse a single model</Novelty>; <Novelty>rejection-sampling fine-tuning for breadth plus PPO for depth</Novelty>; and <Novelty>Ghost Attention (GAtt), a data-side trick that stops multi-turn system-instruction forgetting</Novelty> (§3). Plus the released artifacts: Llama 2 base (7B/13B/70B; 2T tokens, 4k context, <Term def="grouped-query attention — several query heads share one key/value head, shrinking the KV cache for fast large-batch inference">GQA</Term>) and Llama 2-Chat under a commercial license (§1, Tab.1).

## Research questions

1. Can an open, documented pipeline reproduce the helpfulness and safety of closed product LLMs — and what does it actually take (data volumes, reward-model quality, number of iterations)? (§1, §3)
2. Is RLHF a costly add-on or the essential ingredient — what does preference optimization do that supervised fine-tuning cannot? (§3.1, §5.1)
3. Can safety be improved aggressively without taxing helpfulness? (§4.2)

## Methodology

Pretrain a Llama-1-style transformer, longer and wider in the ways that matter for serving (2T tokens, doubled 4k context, GQA at 34B/70B — each ablated in App.A.2.1). Then align in three stages (Fig.4): SFT on a small curated set, then iterative RLHF (V1→V5) driven by two reward models trained on ~1.4M in-house preference comparisons, mixing rejection-sampling fine-tuning with PPO. Safety gets its own parallel track: safety SFT, a safety reward model, and targeted <Term def="prefix a safety preprompt to get a safer answer, then fine-tune on that answer without the preprompt, distilling the prefix into the weights">context distillation</Term>, audited by 350+ red-teamers (§4).

<Pass title="Method details — SFT, reward models, RLHF iterations, GAtt, ablations">
**SFT (§3.1):** bootstrap from public instruction data, then replace scale with curation: 27,540 vendor-annotated prompt+response pairs. Cosine LR 2e−5, 2 epochs, 4096 sequence length, loss only on answer tokens.

**Preference data (§3.2.1, Tab.6):** annotators write a prompt, pick the better of two responses sampled from different model variants/temperatures, and grade the gap on a 4-point scale (significantly better → negligibly better). Helpfulness and safety are collected under separate guidelines; safety pairs also get a 3-way label (safe-vs-unsafe 18%, both safe 47%, both unsafe 35%). 1,418,091 Meta comparisons (longer and more multi-turn than any open preference set), plus ~1.5M open-source comparisons.

**Reward models (§3.2.2):** initialized *from the chat model checkpoint* so the RM "knows what the chat model knows" (prevents rewarding hallucinations it cannot detect), with the LM head swapped for a scalar head. Loss = binary ranking with a rating-dependent margin:

<MathBlock>{`\\mathcal{L}_{\\text{ranking}} = -\\log\\Big(\\sigma\\big(r_\\theta(x, y_c) - r_\\theta(x, y_r) - m(r)\\big)\\Big)`}</MathBlock>

where <M tex="m(r)" /> grows with how separable the pair is — this margin measurably improves helpfulness-RM accuracy on distinct pairs (Tab.28, App.A.3.3). One epoch only (more overfits). Helpfulness RM trains on all Meta Helpfulness + balanced Safety/open-source data; Safety RM on all Meta Safety + Anthropic Harmless with 10% helpfulness data, which specifically helps accuracy on both-responses-safe pairs.

**RLHF (§3.2.3):** five iterations. Rejection sampling (70B only; smaller models are fine-tuned on the 70B's selected samples — a distillation): sample K responses per prompt, fine-tune on the argmax-reward one. PPO on top from V4 onward, with the piecewise reward — safety RM score if the prompt is safety-tagged or the safety score falls below 0.15 (precision 0.89 / recall 0.55), else helpfulness RM — whitened via logit, plus a KL penalty (<M tex="\beta = 0.01" /> at 7B/13B, 0.005 at 34B/70B) to prevent reward hacking. A found-the-hard-way lesson: restricting rejection sampling to the *latest* model's samples caused capability regressions by V3 (e.g., rhyming), fixed by pooling top samples from all prior iterations.

**GAtt (§3.3):** to stop system instructions ("act as Napoleon", "answer in haiku") being forgotten after a few turns, synthetically append the instruction to *every* user turn of a dialogue, sample responses, then train with the instruction kept only in the first turn — zeroing the loss on all earlier turns to avoid a train/inference mismatch. Attention maps show the system message stays attended for many more turns (Fig.10); control holds beyond 20 turns (App.A.3.5).

**Architecture ablations (App.A.2.1):** at fixed 150B tokens, 4k context beats 2k on long-input tasks (SCROLLS; NarrativeQA F1 0.21 → 17.26) with no loss on standard tasks (Tab.16–17); GQA matches MHA quality while MQA trails on average (Tab.18), and GQA/MQA sustain much larger inference batch sizes (MHA hits OOM where they run, Fig.24) — hence GQA for 34B/70B.
</Pass>

## Main result

On human evaluation over ~4,000 helpfulness prompts with 3 raters each, <Finding src="Fig.12, §3.4.2">Llama 2-Chat 70B is competitive with ChatGPT (36% win / 31.5% tie), beats PaLM-bison by a large margin, and the 34B model wins more than 75% against Vicuna-33B and Falcon-40B</Finding>. On safety, <Finding src="Fig.17–18, §4.4">Llama 2-Chat shows the lowest violation rates across sizes on ~2,000 adversarial prompts, single- and multi-turn</Finding>. The base model leads all open models on academic benchmarks — <Finding src="Tab.3">Llama 2 70B: MMLU 68.9 (+5 over Llama 1 65B), BBH 51.2 (+8)</Finding> — and sits near GPT-3.5 except on code:

## Key findings

- <Finding src="§3.1">SFT quality beats quantity: setting aside millions of third-party examples for 27,540 curated annotations "notably improved" results — and the SFT model's own outputs soon rivaled human-written data</Finding>, which is why annotation budget was reallocated from demonstrations to preferences.
- <Finding src="§5.1, Fig.20">RLHF surpasses SFT because humans compare better than they write: SFT inherits the tail of bad annotations and is capped by the best annotator's skill, while preference-driven reward pushes the response distribution past both</Finding> — the paper's provocative framing: "supervised data may no longer be the gold standard."
- <Finding src="Tab.7, Fig.6">The in-house reward models beat all public RMs *and* zero-shot GPT-4 at judging Meta preference data (Helpfulness RM 63.2 vs GPT-4 58.6), and RM accuracy scales with data and size without saturating</Finding> — treated as *the* proxy metric: RM gains translate directly into chat-model gains.
- <Finding src="Fig.15, §4.2.3">Safety data scaling is nearly free: raising safety data from 0% to 100% (~0.1M examples) sharply improves the safety score distribution while mean helpfulness stays flat</Finding>; the tax shows up only as false refusals — ~0.05% on the helpfulness set but substantial on a 210-prompt borderline set ("Christmas Crack" tripping the word "crack"-adjacent pattern) (App.Fig.33, Tab.41).
- <Finding src="Fig.8, Fig.21, §5.1">RLHF dynamically rescales temperature per prompt type: factual prompts converge to the same answer even at high temperature while creative prompts keep diversity</Finding> — sampling hyperparameters must be re-tuned every RLHF iteration (optimal T rises to ~1.2–1.3).
- <Finding src="Fig.22–23, Tab.15">Alignment surfaces latent abilities: zero-shot multi-tool use emerges without any tool-use training (with a calculator: 67.1/69.2/82.4 on ASDiv/SVAMP/MAWPS vs Toolformer's 40.4/29.4/44.0), and 1,000 date-tagged SFT examples suffice for robust temporal organization of knowledge</Finding>.
- <Finding src="App.A.6, Tab.51">A token-level skipgram contamination audit (10+-token matches, 4-token slack) finds only HellaSwag and MMLU-Humanities meaningfully boosted by contamination — with the 70B benefiting more than the 7B</Finding>; other benchmarks show no significant effect.

## Implications

The open ecosystem got its alignment textbook: preference-data volumes, RM training mixes, iteration counts, and the failure modes (RM staleness, rejection-sampling forgetting, false refusals) that closed labs had kept internal. <Related>It operationalizes the InstructGPT/Anthropic HH line of RLHF work at open scale, supersedes LLaMA-1, and is the direct substrate for the Code Llama / open fine-tune wave; its two-RM safety design anticipates later safety-classifier stacks, and its "humans compare better than they write" argument is the practical case later formalized by DPO-style direct preference methods</Related>. <FollowUp>Whether the pricey PPO stage is necessary at all (DPO), whether iterative on-distribution annotation can be replaced by AI feedback (RLAIF), and how the false-refusal/borderline trade-off should be measured as a first-class metric</FollowUp>.

## Limitations

- <Caveat>English-only in practice: 89.7% of pretraining data is English (Tab.10) and all testing was in English; non-English performance is called "fragile"</Caveat> (§5.2).
- <Caveat>The helpfulness human eval's ~4k prompts include no coding or reasoning prompts, and only the final turn of multi-turn dialogues is judged</Caveat> (§3.4.2) — exactly where Tab.4 shows the biggest closed-model gap (HumanEval 29.9 vs GPT-3.5's 48.1).
- <Caveat>Safety evaluations use Meta's own content standards and prompt set, which the paper concedes are "likely biased towards Llama 2-Chat"</Caveat> (Fig.3 caption, §4.4).
- <Caveat>Safety tuning overshoots at times: over-cautious refusals and safety boilerplate are acknowledged</Caveat> (§5.2), and the 34B model was withheld for lack of red-teaming time (§1).
- <Caveat>Inter-rater reliability on helpfulness is modest (Gwet's AC2 0.37–0.55), so close comparisons (vs ChatGPT) are noisy</Caveat> (§3.4.2).

## Critiques and Questions

1. The headline "on par with ChatGPT" rests on a prompt set that excludes the categories where the model is weakest (code, reasoning); the academic tables and the human eval quietly measure different models.
2. The pipeline is documented but not reproducible: the 1.4M preference comparisons — by the paper's own logic the decisive asset — are not released, so the community gets the recipe without the key ingredient.
3. Reward models serve as both training signal and primary evaluator (Fig.11 left); the Goodhart risk is acknowledged and partially hedged with GPT-4-as-judge, but GPT-4 win-rates and human win-rates are never reconciled quantitatively.
4. There is no controlled comparison of rejection sampling vs PPO vs both — the sequential adoption (RS until V4, then +PPO) confounds algorithm choice with data iteration, so "what did PPO buy" is unanswerable from the paper.
5. Emergent tool use is demonstrated with an illustrative figure and one calculator benchmark; "never trained on tool use" is hard to audit given 2T undisclosed pretraining tokens.
