<TLDR>OpenChat's C-RLFT aligns an open-source LLM using ordinary mixed-quality SFT data (ShareGPT: ~6k GPT-4 conversations mixed with ~64k GPT-3.5 ones) by treating the *data source itself* as a coarse reward and conditioning the policy on source-specific prompt tokens — a single-stage, RL-free, preference-label-free weighted SFT that makes a llama-2-13b the best-scoring 13B open model of its time (77.3 avg win rate, above Claude's 76.6).</TLDR>

<WhyItMatters>Between "just SFT on distilled conversations" (Vicuna) and "full RLHF" (Llama-2-chat), this paper stakes out the practical middle: quality signal is already in your dataset's metadata, and conditioning + reweighting extracts it for free. It imports offline-RL ideas (reward-weighted regression, goal-conditioned SL) into LLM post-training, and its inference trick — prompt as the expert class — anticipates quality-steering via system prompts.</WhyItMatters>

## Motivation
Open-source alignment in 2023 had two doors, both partly stuck. Door one, SFT on collected conversations (Vicuna-style ShareGPT): <Problem>these datasets are inherently mixed-quality — a sliver of expert (GPT-4) responses inside a mass of sub-optimal (GPT-3.5) ones — and SFT's maximum-likelihood objective imitates all of it uniformly, so the low-quality mass drags the model down</Problem> (§1, §2). Door two, RLFT/RLHF: <Problem>it needs high-quality *pairwise or ranking* preference annotations plus reward-model training and RL optimization — expensive human labeling and unstable multi-stage training that most open efforts cannot afford</Problem> (§1, §2; even DPO-style single-stage methods still require the preference pairs). The data everyone already has carries no preference labels — but it does carry *source labels*, and nobody was using them.

## Contribution (what's new)
<Novelty>Treat data provenance as a coarse-grained reward — expert source (GPT-4) gets reward 1, sub-optimal source (GPT-3.5) gets α below 1 — and, because such a reward is far too crude to optimize directly, pair it with a class-conditioned policy <M tex="\pi_\theta(y \mid x, c)" /> that is KL-regularized toward the class-conditioned behavior policy <M tex="\pi_c" /> of the dataset instead of the pretrained model <M tex="\pi_0" /></Novelty> (§3, Eq.3–4). Both replacements are forced: the reward alone cannot rank two responses *within* a class, so the class token must inject the missing quality information into the input; and regularizing toward <M tex="\pi_0" /> would anchor the model to something *worse* than even the sub-optimal data's generator (base llama-2 vs GPT-3.5). <Novelty>The KL-regularized optimum then collapses, via the standard closed form, into single-stage reward-weighted regression — plain SFT with per-class weights and source-tagged prompts — no reward model, no RL, no reference model in memory during training</Novelty> (Eq.5–6).

## Research questions
1. Can quality *metadata* (which model generated this conversation) substitute for preference labels in aligning open LLMs? (§3)
2. Does exploiting the sub-optimal majority beat simply filtering down to the expert slice? (§5.2)
3. Does the resulting model generalize (AGIEval) rather than overfit chat-style benchmarks? (§4.2)

## Methodology
Tag every ShareGPT conversation with its source class using distinct conversation templates — expert turns get `GPT4 User:` / `GPT4 Assistant:`, sub-optimal turns get `GPT3 User:` / `GPT3 Assistant:`, with a new `<|end of turn|>` special token separating utterances (§3.2). Fine-tune llama-2-13b on all ~70k conversations with a weighted log-likelihood: weight 1.0 on GPT-4 examples, 0.1 on GPT-3.5 examples (the class-constant simplification of <M tex="\exp(r_c/\beta)" />, §4.1). At inference, always present the GPT-4 template, steering generation into the expert-conditioned distribution (§3.2).

<Pass title="Method details: derivation, setup, and evaluation">
**Derivation** (Eq.4–6, App.B): maximize <M tex="\mathbb{E}_{y\sim\pi}[r_c(x,y)] - \beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}(\pi, \pi_c)" />. The KKT conditions give the Gibbs form <M tex="\pi^*(y\mid x,c) \propto \pi_c(y\mid x,c)\exp(r_c(x,y)/\beta)" />. Projecting <M tex="\pi^*" /> onto the parametric policy by minimizing forward KL over the dataset — where <M tex="\pi_c" /> is by construction the dataset's own class-conditional distribution — yields

<MathBlock>{`\\pi_\\theta = \\arg\\max_\\theta\\; \\mathbb{E}_{(x,y,c)\\sim\\mathcal{D}_c}\\Big[\\exp\\big(\\tfrac{1}{\\beta} r_c(x,y)\\big)\\, \\log \\pi_\\theta(y \\mid x, c)\\Big]`}</MathBlock>

i.e. reward-weighted regression (the AWR/Peters-Schaal line, imported from offline RL). Unlike PPO-based RLHF, no reference model is kept in memory during training — after initializing from <M tex="\pi_0" />, only one network trains (§3.2).

**Conditioning implementation** (App.A, Tab.3, Fig.7): the class token must be repeated *every turn* (before speaker or before assistant — similar performance); conditioning only once at conversation start is much worse, consistent with LLMs forgetting early prompts over long contexts (the Llama-2 multi-turn-consistency observation).

**Training** (§4.1): 5 epochs, sequence length 4,096, effective batch 200k tokens, AdamW (β1 0.9, β2 0.95, weight decay 0.1), cosine schedule peaking at 6.7e-5 — pretraining-style hyperparameters with lr scaled by the square root of batch size.

**Evaluation** (§4.1, App.C): instruction following via three LLM-judged benchmarks — AlpacaEval (805 instructions, win rate vs text-davinci-003), MT-bench (80 multi-turn questions; win rate vs gpt-3.5-turbo), Vicuna-bench (80 questions, win rate vs gpt-3.5-turbo) — plus AGIEval English multiple-choice exams (SAT, LSAT, LogiQA, AQuA-RAT), zero-shot, accuracy-scored, as the generalization check. Judge robustness probed with gpt-3.5 and claude-2 as alternate evaluators (App.E/H).
</Pass>

## Main result
<Finding src="Tab.1">openchat-13b reaches a 77.3 average win rate — the best of all 13B open models, above Claude (76.6) and far above gpt-3.5-turbo's own average (62.0) — winning AlpacaEval (89.5) and MT-bench (57.5) outright and placing second on Vicuna-bench (85.0 vs llama-2-chat-13b's 86.9)</Finding>. It does this with ~70k unlabeled conversations, where llama-2-chat-13b used ~27k curated SFT examples plus ~2.9M preference annotations and RLHF (Tab.4).

## Key findings
- <Finding src="Tab.2">The class-conditioned policy is the load-bearing component: removing conditioning drops the average from 77.3 to 60.5, while removing the reward weights costs only 3.3 points (74.0)</Finding> — the quality signal is delivered mostly through the *input tokens*, not the loss weights.
- <Finding src="Tab.2">Quality beats quantity, but exploiting beats filtering: SFT on only the ~6k GPT-4 conversations (64.5) crushes SFT on all ~70k (52.7) and on the ~64k GPT-3.5 subset (42.8) — yet C-RLFT on everything (77.3) beats even the clean-data SFT by 12.8 points</Finding>; the sub-optimal majority contains real signal if the model can tell it apart. Scaling naive SFT data to 125k (vicuna-v1.5, 54.4) barely helps.
- <Finding src="Fig.2b, Tab.5">On AGIEval, openchat-13b (36.4) is the *only* fine-tuned model to surpass its base llama-2-13b (35.0); llama-2-chat-13b falls to 25.8 and vicuna-v1.5 to 20.7</Finding> — chat fine-tuning typically pays a capability/forgetting tax that C-RLFT avoids.
- <Finding src="Fig.4, Fig.5">The model demonstrably internalizes the quality classes: UMAP of its representations separates GPT-4 from GPT-3.5 conversations (SFT-only representations are intermingled), and swapping the inference prompt from the GPT4 to the GPT3 template causes a substantial performance drop</Finding> (§5.3) — conditioning works as designed, and the class token is a controllable quality dial.
- <Finding src="Fig.6">Sub-sampling the ~6k expert conversations hurts more than sub-sampling the 10x-larger GPT-3.5 pool</Finding> (§5.4) — scarce expert data is the binding resource.
- <Finding src="App.E/H, Fig.8-9">The ranking is judge-robust: with gpt-3.5 and claude-2 as evaluators (inter-judge Pearson r = 0.91; r = 0.79/0.73 vs gpt-4), openchat-13b still tops all 13B open models</Finding> — partially defusing the GPT-4-judges-GPT-4-distilled-style worry.

## Implications
The paper reframes "clean your SFT data" as "condition on your SFT data's quality structure": provenance metadata is a free, weak reward, and <Related>offline-RL machinery (KL-regularized reward maximization, reward-weighted regression, goal/outcome-conditioned supervised learning à la Decision Transformer / RvS)</Related> is exactly the right tool to consume it. It sits beside <Related>DPO</Related> as the second 2023 demonstration that the RLHF objective's closed-form optimum lets you skip RL — but under weaker supervision (class labels instead of preference pairs). Practically it fed the OpenChat model line (later openchat-3.5 on Mistral) and legitimized weighted/conditioned SFT in open post-training stacks. <FollowUp>Finer-grained conditioning — per-example quality scores instead of binary source classes — is the obvious extension the authors themselves flag</FollowUp>; <FollowUp>whether conditioning tokens can steer other latent attributes (style, safety tier) at inference</FollowUp> and <FollowUp>how C-RLFT composes with preference-based methods like DPO when some pairs exist</FollowUp> are open threads.

## Limitations
- <Caveat>Source-equals-quality is an approximation the authors call "overly simplistic": every GPT-4 response counts as expert and every GPT-3.5 response as sub-optimal, with a single global weight (α, in practice 0.1) chosen without reported sensitivity analysis</Caveat> (§7).
- <Caveat>Two quality classes, one dataset (ShareGPT), one base model (llama-2-13b): generality across class counts, data mixes, and scales is asserted but untested</Caveat> (§4.1).
- <Caveat>The headline benchmarks are all LLM-judged, short-form instruction following; AlpacaEval's comparison target is the weak text-davinci-003, and AGIEval is the only ground-truth-scored evaluation</Caveat>. The model is trained on OpenAI-model outputs and judged mostly by an OpenAI model — evaluator-consistency checks (r = 0.91 between alternate judges) mitigate but cannot eliminate style-matching bias.
- <Caveat>Improving reasoning is explicitly out of scope — the AGIEval gain over base is 1.4 points, so C-RLFT mostly *avoids* regression rather than adding capability</Caveat> (§7).
- <Caveat>No safety/harmlessness evaluation at all — unlike the RLHF pipelines it replaces, which were built partly for that purpose</Caveat> (inferred; Not reported).

## Critiques and Questions
1. The theory-practice gap is wider than the paper admits: the derivation optimizes reward under a KL to <M tex="\pi_c" />, but the implementation is exactly class-weighted SFT with tagged prompts. Any weighting scheme admits a post-hoc reward interpretation; the KKT derivation lends elegance, not evidence. The ablations, not the math, carry the claim.
2. Since removing reward weights costs only 3.3 points, the mechanism is mostly "tell the model who wrote this" — which makes the comparison to a simple baseline conspicuously absent: SFT on all data with source tags but *no* weights and no RL framing (their "w/o reward" is close, at 74.0, quietly confirming this).
3. Distillation ceiling: everything the model learns comes from GPT-3.5/GPT-4 outputs, so the method cannot exceed its teachers on substance — the 89.5 AlpacaEval number measures style-and-helpfulness match against a weak target, and the gpt-4 row (89.3 average) still dominates every open model on the harder MT-bench.
4. Multi-turn conversations mixing quality levels within one dialogue, near-duplicate prompts across classes, and contamination between ShareGPT and the judged benchmarks are not analyzed.
