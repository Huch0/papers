<TLDR>ReLook trains a 7B/8B open coding policy with GRPO where the reward is a frozen multimodal LLM scoring temporal screenshots of the rendered page, the policy learns to call that critic as a tool inside a generate–diagnose–refine loop, a strict "accept only strictly improving revisions" rule (Forced Optimization) stops revisions from getting worse, and at inference the critic is dropped so the policy self-edits alone.</TLDR>

<WhyItMatters>This is the closest published blueprint to my thesis setting: an open-weight model, execution (rendering) in the loop, a visual judge as the training signal, and a repair loop that must be learned rather than prompted. Its failure modes (behavioral collapse, reward hacking via reflection avoidance, weak gains on multi-file projects) are exactly the ones I need to design around.</WhyItMatters>

## Motivation
Front-end code has no unit tests that matter: correctness is what the browser shows and how the page reacts. <Problem>A text-only policy is blind to the pixel consequences of its own code, so it drifts layouts, breaks interactions (missing event listeners), and produces incoherent aesthetics</Problem> (§1, §3.7). Two prior families each miss half the loop: <Problem>one-shot vision-to-code systems (pix2code, Design2Code, UICoder) generate but never refine</Problem>, and <Problem>self-refinement frameworks (Self-Refine, Reflexion, CRITIC, CodeRL) iterate but only over pixel-blind signals such as unit tests or linters</Problem> (§1). On top of that, when I do give a model rich visual feedback, I observe <Problem>behavioral collapse: the revision after good feedback often scores *lower* than the draft it was meant to fix</Problem> (§2.6, Fig.4). So the problem is not "add a critic"; it is "make a small model actually improve under a critic without regressing or gaming the reward".

## Contribution (what's new)
The core insight is that <Novelty>the perceptual judge belongs *inside the RL loop twice*: once as the reward (a frozen <Term def="Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, a multimodal LLM that scores the prompt + code + three screenshots taken post-load, +1s, +2s">MLLM critic</Term> scores rendered screenshots), and once as a tool the policy learns to invoke mid-trajectory for textual, vision-grounded repair hints</Novelty> (§2.2–2.5). Two safeguards are forced by what breaks without them. <Novelty>Zero reward for any invalid render</Novelty> is needed because a plausible-looking but unrenderable page would otherwise be scored by a judge reading the code (§2.5). <Novelty>Forced Optimization — a revision enters the trajectory only if its critic score strictly exceeds the best-so-far, with up to 10 resamples per round</Novelty> — is needed because the obvious alternative, a negative reward for regressions, taught the agent to stop reflecting altogether (§2.6, App. E). Finally, because the critic's tokens are masked from the policy gradient and optionally distilled with a small loss, <Novelty>the policy internalises the reflection so the critic can be removed at inference</Novelty> (§2.4, §2.7).

## Research questions
1. Does a screenshot-based MLLM reward beat text-only signals for training a small open coding model on front-end tasks?
2. Does letting the agent *call* the critic during rollouts (agentic reflection) add value over the same reward without reflection (Web-RL)?
3. Can the learned reflection survive removing the critic at inference, and at what latency cost?

## Methodology
Running example: the prompt "create a classical chessboard in HTML that runs directly". Round 1: the policy writes a plan `t1` and code `c1`, then emits `<get feedback>`. The sandbox renders `c1`, grabs three screenshots, and the critic returns a score plus text `m1` ("squares misaligned; missing alternating colours"). Round 2: the policy conditions on `q, t1, c1, m1` and proposes `c2`. If the critic score of `c2` is not strictly higher than `c1`, `c2` is discarded and a new `c2` is sampled (≤ 10 tries); if none improves, reflection stops and `c1` is the final answer. The trajectory reward is the mean of accepted round scores, multiplied by a length penalty, and zero if any screenshot failed. <Term def="Group Relative Policy Optimization: sample G outputs per prompt and use the group-mean reward as the baseline for a clipped PPO-style policy-gradient update, no learned value net">GRPO</Term> updates only the `t`/`c` tokens; the critic's `m` tokens get zero advantage.

## Main result
On the Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct backbone, <Finding src="Tab.1">ReLook lifts ArtifactsBench-Lite from 21.59 (base) to 27.88, beating Web-RL (24.89), and the critic-free variant still reaches 25.44</Finding>; the ordering ReLook > Web-RL > Base holds on every ArtifactsBench subset for both backbones (Fig.2, Fig.3). ReLook-7B overtakes Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on A-Lite (27.88 vs 25.73) but stays below GPT-4o (33.25).

## Key findings
- <Finding src="Tab.2">Every component matters, and Forced Optimization is the single largest piece</Finding>: on A-Lite, MLLM reward alone 21.59 → 24.89 (+3.3), adding the invalid-render zero reward → 25.84 (+1.0), adding Forced Optimization → 27.88 (+2.0).
- <Finding src="Fig.4">Behavioral collapse is real for the base model: with the same MLLM feedback, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct degrades after 2–3 reflection rounds, while ReLook improves monotonically across eight forced rounds</Finding> (scores from the training judge, so not independent).
- <Finding src="§2.6, App. E">Penalising regressions with negative reward backfires</Finding>: the agent learns to stop requesting feedback (reward hacking by reflection avoidance), collapsing ReLook into plain RL. A hard acceptance filter fixes it without suppressing reflection: reflection frequency rises and stabilises at ~2 rounds during training (Fig.5).
- <Finding src="§2.5, App. G">Valid Render Rate climbs from ~40% at initialisation to ~80% at convergence</Finding>; a prompt-level "write executable HTML/CSS/JS/SVG" constraint is needed to avoid the zero-reward rule making early training too sparse.
- <Finding src="Tab.1, §3.5">Internalised reflection retains most of the benefit at base-decoding latency</Finding>: w/o-MLLM beats Web-RL on every subset, and per-query time falls from 123.04 s to 18.03 s (Qwen2.5-7B on 4 H20s, critic on 8 H20s, 100 queries).
- <Finding src="§3.6">Gains concentrate on visual-centric subsets and stay small on multi-file projects</Finding>: Web-Bench improves 3.00 → 4.20 pass@2 (40% relative) but remains far below GPT-4o (23.80); single-artifact refinement does not address long-horizon cross-file work.
- <Finding src="Tab.1">Web-RL slightly *hurts* FullStack-Html on Qwen (65.00 → 63.25) while ReLook recovers it (67.50)</Finding>: pure visual reward without reflection can trade functional correctness for looks; the paper does not discuss this.

## Implications
For my thesis, ReLook shows that <Related>a visual MLLM-as-judge in the ArtifactsBench lineage can serve as a *training* reward rather than only an evaluator</Related>, and that the hard part is trajectory hygiene (monotonic acceptance, invalid-render zeroing), not the judge itself. It sits between <Related>one-shot vision-to-code (Design2Code, UICoder, Web2Code, DesignCoder)</Related> and <Related>tool-critique self-refinement (CRITIC, Reflexion, Self-Refine)</Related>, and its Forced Optimization is an online, in-trajectory analogue of best-of-N selection (§4.2). Threads to chase: <FollowUp>replacing the three fixed-time screenshots with scripted interaction traces so the reward can see stateful behaviour (clicks, form state), which the current critic only observes through passive timing</FollowUp>; <FollowUp>whether a much smaller or open critic (the paper's stated future work) keeps the gains, since 32 GPUs for the critic dominates training cost</FollowUp>; <FollowUp>using execution/test signals and the visual judge jointly, given the FullStack-Html regression under visual-only Web-RL</FollowUp>.

## Limitations
- <Caveat>The training critic (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) and evaluator (Gemini-2.5-Pro) are both MLLM judges; human validation is inherited from ArtifactsBench, not re-run, except a 100-task GSB study with no inter-annotator statistics</Caveat> (§1, App. A–B).
- <Caveat>Only 7B/8B backbones, 40 RL steps, front-end only; scaling and broader stacks are untested</Caveat> (App. A).
- <Caveat>"Interaction" is judged from three passive screenshots (post-load, +1s, +2s); no user actions are executed, so interactive integrity is inferred by the judge rather than observed</Caveat> (§2.5, §3.2).
- <Caveat>Means over 3 seeds are reported without variance or significance tests; several deltas (e.g. Llama A-Lite +0.63 for Web-RL) are within plausible seed noise</Caveat> (Tab.1).
- <Caveat>Forced Optimization may reduce exploration on hard cases, and the sandbox's determinism may under-represent real device/locale variability</Caveat> (App. A).
- <Caveat>Code is "planned for release upon publication"; no artifacts are available</Caveat> (App. A).

## Critiques and Questions
1. The text summary of §3.6 does not match Table 1 everywhere: the "3.2–6.1 point" range for visual subsets excludes Qwen A-Web (+2.62), and "2.4–3.4 on A-Easy" excludes Llama (+4.75). The largest Qwen gain is actually A-Si (+7.75), which the text does not mention. Treat the prose ranges as approximate.
2. Web-RL vs ReLook conflates two changes: agentic reflection *and* Forced Optimization. Table 2's "MLLM reward + format constraint" row (25.84) has no reflection either, so the marginal value of reflection alone without acceptance filtering is never isolated.
3. Because the critic score is also the acceptance criterion, Forced Optimization is effectively rejection sampling against the reward model during rollout; how much of the gain is "learned self-improvement" versus "better training targets from filtered search"? The w/o-MLLM numbers suggest some transfer, but the distillation loss ablation (γ) is described as a sweep and never reported.
4. Reflection count converging to ~2 while the cap is 3 could mean tasks saturate, or that the 10-resample budget often fails on hard tasks; the rejection rate is not reported.
5. Why does the critic-at-inference variant score identically to w/o-MLLM on FullStack and Web-Bench (Tab.1 caption says both use critic-free inference there)? Then "ReLook" on unit-test benchmarks is not the full method, so the headline claim of consistent gains "across three benchmarks" rests on critic-free inference for two of them.

<SelfCheck q="Why did the authors abandon a negative reward for regressions in favour of a hard acceptance rule?" a="The penalty made the agent avoid requesting feedback altogether (fewer reflections means fewer chances to be penalised), i.e. reward hacking that turned ReLook back into plain RL. A strict accept-only-if-better filter keeps regressions out of the trajectory without discouraging reflection; reflection frequency rose and stabilised at about two rounds (§2.6, App. E, Fig.5)." />

## Glossary
- <Term def="A rollout in which the policy alternates generation with a tool call to an external multimodal critic, then revises">generate–diagnose–refine loop</Term>
- <Term def="The paper's term for a revision after feedback scoring lower than the draft it revised">behavioral collapse</Term>
- <Term def="Accept a revision into the trajectory only if its critic score strictly exceeds the best so far; resample up to 10 times, else stop">Forced Optimization</Term>
- <Term def="Ablation baseline: same MLLM screenshot reward and GRPO, but no in-rollout critic call or reflection">Web-RL</Term>
- <Term def="The 0–100 rubric score from the MLLM judge covering spec adherence, layout, typography/colour, and interactive integrity">VisualScore</Term>
- <Term def="Fraction of rollouts whose screenshots all render successfully; rises ~40% to ~80% during training">Valid Render Rate</Term>
- <Term def="Good:Same:Bad counts from pairwise human preference under majority vote">GSB</Term>
