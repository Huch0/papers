<TLDR>Princeton (HAL group) decomposes agent reliability into twelve accuracy-independent metrics along four safety-critical-engineering dimensions — consistency, robustness, predictability, safety — and profiles 15 models (2024–2026) on GAIA and τ-bench, finding that accuracy rose at ~0.22–0.23/yr while overall reliability rose at only 0.03/yr (GAIA, p=0.08) to 0.09/yr (τ-bench).</TLDR>

<WhyItMatters>Squarely in my agent-evaluation-methodology lane (ICML 2026 advisor-review pick): it operationalizes exactly the things single-number agent benchmarks hide — run-to-run variance (pass@k vs pass∧k), paraphrase/format sensitivity, and confidence validity — as a reusable multi-run/multi-condition protocol on the HAL harness. The perturbation and K-run design is directly transferable to my own harness work.</WhyItMatters>

## Motivation
<Problem>The dominant agent-evaluation paradigm compresses behavior into a single mean success rate, which cannot distinguish an agent that fails predictably on a fixed task subset from one that fails randomly at the same rate, nor benign formatting errors from catastrophic ones like deleting files</Problem> (§1). High-profile deployment failures (Replit's assistant deleting a production database, Operator's unauthorized purchase, the NYC business chatbot giving illegal advice) came from agents that looked capable in internal evaluation; an Anthropic survey of 80k+ people found unreliability the most-cited AI concern. <Problem>ML research treats prompt sensitivity, inference nondeterminism, calibration, and harm evaluation as isolated phenomena, with no unified, computable reliability framework</Problem> — in stark contrast to aviation/nuclear/automotive practice, where reliability is a certifiable, multi-dimensional property with explicit tail-risk quantification (§2, Tab.1).

## Contribution (what's new)
The delta that survives deleting "we propose": <Novelty>a HELM-style unification — four reliability dimensions distilled from cross-domain safety standards, instantiated as twelve concrete metrics that are computable from ordinary benchmark runs and designed to be independent of raw accuracy</Novelty> (consistency normalizes variance by max Bernoulli variance; robustness uses perturbed/baseline accuracy ratios). On top of it, <Novelty>the first longitudinal reliability profile of frontier agents: 15 models spanning 24 months, showing per-dimension where progress is real (calibration, safety on τ-bench) and where it is absent (consistency, discrimination)</Novelty> (Fig.8). The framing must take this decomposed form because a single aggregate — like accuracy itself — would re-hide the uneven progress it exposes.

## Research questions
Has agent reliability improved alongside capability over recent model generations? Which of the four dimensions lag, and do scale or reasoning training help? Can qualitative safety-critical principles be translated into metrics measurable with today's benchmarks?

## Methodology
Two complementary benchmarks under one protocol: <Term def="General AI Assistants benchmark: open-ended web browsing + file + code tasks; 165 validation tasks in 3 difficulty levels; exact-match grading; humans score >90%">GAIA</Term> (ReAct-style HAL generalist scaffold) and <Term def="multi-turn airline customer-service simulation with tool calls against a database; success = final DB state matches target; restricted here to the 26 of 50 airline tasks Cuadron et al. 2025 verified as correctly labeled">τ-bench</Term> (tool-calling scaffold). Each task runs K=5 times at temperature 0 (reasoning models: provider defaults), so residual variance is non-sampling stochasticity (floating-point non-associativity, batch effects). Perturbation arms: J=5 GPT-4o-generated naturalistic paraphrases (Rprompt), fault injection at p=0.2 over 7 fault types (Rfault), medium-intensity tool/format perturbations like camelCase keys and date-format shifts (Renv). Confidence is post-hoc self-assessment (0–100 with full trace in context); safety is a GPT-4o judge checking benchmark-specific constraints and scoring severity (§4.1, App.F).

<MathBlock>{`C_{\\text{out}} = \\tfrac{1}{T}\\textstyle\\sum_{t}(2\\hat{p}_t - 1)^2, \\qquad R = \\tfrac{1}{3}(R_{\\text{Con}} + R_{\\text{Pred}} + R_{\\text{Rob}}), \\qquad R_{\\text{Saf}} = 1-(1-S_{\\text{comp}})(1-S_{\\text{harm}})`}</MathBlock>

Safety is deliberately excluded from aggregate R: violations are a tail phenomenon that averaging would mask (§3.5).

## Main result
Capability and reliability have decoupled. On GAIA, accuracy improves at <Finding src="Fig.7">+0.23/yr (r=0.80) while overall reliability improves at +0.03/yr (r=0.46, p=0.08 — not significant)</Finding>; on τ-bench, +0.22/yr accuracy vs +0.09/yr reliability (r=0.86). Regressing reliability on accuracy gives slopes of only <Finding src="Fig.7-8">0.18 (GAIA, r=0.76) and 0.33 (τ-bench, r=0.92)</Finding> — a point of accuracy buys a fraction of a point of reliability, and less the more open-ended the tasks. Per dimension on GAIA, <Finding src="Fig.8a">consistency (r=−0.14, slope −0.01/yr) and safety (r=−0.17) show no improvement at all over 24 months; only predictability trends up (r=+0.75, +0.09/yr)</Finding>. All three providers cluster together — an industry-wide plateau, not a vendor problem (Fig.1).

## Key findings
- <Finding src="Fig.2, Fig.11">Outcome consistency is the hardest metric everywhere — agents that can solve a task often do not solve it every time</Finding>: the pass@k vs pass∧k gap made concrete; the three newest OpenAI models have the *lowest* overall consistency on GAIA (RCon 0.59–0.62 vs 0.80–0.81 for Claude 3.5 Haiku / Opus 4.5 / 4.7).
- <Finding src="Fig.11, §G.3">A "what but not when" pattern: distributional trajectory consistency far exceeds sequential</Finding> — agents pick the same action *types* across runs but reorder them (e.g. GPT-5.5 on GAIA: Cd 0.71 vs Cs 0.53), which the authors read as unstable planning/execution rather than unstable tool choice.
- <Finding src="Fig.17, Fig.3">Fault and environment robustness are near ceiling (mostly 0.91–1.00), but prompt robustness is the differentiator</Finding>: naturalistic rephrasings alone cost up to a third of accuracy on GAIA (Claude 3 Haiku 0.66, Gemini 2.5 Flash 0.69, GPT-5.2 0.73; Opus 4.5 only 0.77) — models absorb genuine technical failures yet trip on surface-level wording, especially where the environment does not constrain the solution path.
- <Finding src="Fig.4, Fig.12-16">Calibration has genuinely improved (Claude Opus 4.5 hits ECE 0.030 on GAIA; Opus 4.7 is the only under-confident model), but discrimination has not</Finding>: on τ-bench four older models rank failures *above* successes (AUROC 0.36–0.46) and selective-prediction curves are near-random for most models; on GAIA discrimination even regresses in some recent models (Opus 4.7 at 0.63 vs GPT-4 Turbo 0.75). Well-calibrated averages with no per-task warning signal are a false sense of safety.
- <Finding src="Fig.5, Fig.18">Safety violations dropped sharply with model generation (violation rate 0.64 for Claude 3 Haiku vs 0.03–0.05 for Gemini 3.5 Flash / Opus 4.5 / GPT-5.2), with financial accuracy the most common violation class</Finding> — but progress is not monotone (Opus 4.7 compliance 0.88 regresses vs Opus 4.5's 0.96), and constraint-level analysis exists only for τ-bench.
- <Finding src="Fig.9, Fig.19">Reasoning training buys accuracy much faster than reliability: +0.22/+0.24 accuracy (GAIA/τ-bench) vs +0.03/+0.05 outcome consistency, and GAIA discrimination *drops* 0.75→0.69</Finding>; larger models often have *worse* consistency than smaller ones (more solution paths → more run-to-run variance), and benchmark structure matters — the structured τ-bench shows reliability gains that open-ended GAIA does not (§G.2).

## Implications
For evaluation methodology this is a template: reliability requires multi-run protocols (K>1 with variance reporting), generative/parameterized benchmarks (rename fields, inject faults) rather than fixed test sets, and temporal re-evaluation (Rec.1) — and reliability requirements should scale with autonomy: a 90%-accuracy agent is a fine copilot but an unacceptable unattended system (Rec.4). It also shows benchmark quality is a reliability confounder: on the corrected τ-bench subset, measured predictability improves almost universally, because flawed answer keys punish justified confidence as overconfidence (Fig.6) — an argument for <Related>Cuadron et al. 2025-style benchmark verification</Related> before drawing calibration conclusions. Lineage: extends <Related>HELM's multi-metric philosophy (Liang et al. 2022)</Related> to agents, builds on <Related>τ-bench's pass∧k (Yao et al. 2024)</Related>, <Related>Vendrow et al. 2025 (benchmarks track capability, not reliability)</Related>, the <Related>HAL harness (Kapoor et al. 2025)</Related>, and <Related>Thinking Machines' nondeterminism analysis (He 2025)</Related>. <FollowUp>Whether SWE-agent/computer-use scaffolds (Claude Code, Codex — named as planned future work) change the reliability profile; scaffold-level retries and validators could buy consistency that model scaling has not.</FollowUp> <FollowUp>Formalizing operating envelopes / failure semantics for agents (App.D.3's admitted gap vs classical reliability engineering).</FollowUp> <FollowUp>Optimizing directly for these metrics — and how quickly they Goodhart once targeted (the impact statement itself warns metrics can be gamed).</FollowUp>

## Limitations
- <Caveat>Two benchmarks, one scaffold each</Caveat>: GAIA (165 tasks) + τ-bench airline reduced to 26 verified tasks; the authors concede other scaffolds "could yield qualitatively different reliability profiles" (§6) — trend claims ride on 15 model×2 settings.
- <Caveat>Safety numbers come from a GPT-4o judge with no reported human validation or judge-agreement figures</Caveat>; judge validity is itself a reliability concern the authors acknowledge. Total evaluation cost: Not reported.
- <Caveat>Temperature 0 throughout</Caveat>, so measured consistency is an upper bound on what accuracy-maximizing (nonzero-temperature) deployments would see — the authors state this may overestimate achievable reliability.
- <Caveat>Aggregation is subjective</Caveat>: uniform dimension weights, RPred = Brier only, safety excluded from R — so headline "reliability" is one of many possible summaries (they invite alternatives).
- <Caveat>Statistical resolution is thin</Caveat>: K=5 runs, 26 τ-bench tasks, AUROC over ~26 outcomes; error bars are ±1 SEM clipped to [0,1], and no confidence intervals are given for the reliability metrics themselves — cross-model deltas of 0.03–0.05 are within noise.
- <Caveat>Scope excludes adversarial robustness and alignment</Caveat>; "safety" means bounded operational severity only (§1 Scope).

1. **Consistent failure counts as consistency.** Claude 3 Haiku ties for the best GAIA outcome consistency (0.84) while having the worst compliance (0.36) and low accuracy — `(2p̂−1)²` rewards determinism in either direction. The dashboard-level reading the paper encourages ("consistency stagnated") conflates models becoming *less deterministic* with models moving from always-fail toward sometimes-succeed; a capability-conditioned consistency (e.g. among solved tasks) would disentangle this. The authors notice the mechanism in the difficulty analysis (§G.6) but not in the headline trend.
2. **The fault-robustness ceiling is partly built into the protocol.** With recovery probability 0.3+0.2i per attempt, an injected fault survives all three retries only ~10.5% of the time (~2% of calls at p=0.2). Near-1.0 Rfault may say more about the injector's leniency than about agent fault-handling; the "models handle technical failures gracefully" contrast with prompt fragility (§4.2) is weaker than it reads.
3. **One judge, many roles.** GPT-4o generates the paraphrases, and grades safety/severity — the same family as an evaluated model, with no inter-judge or human agreement reported. If the judge systematically under-flags its own family's violation style, provider comparisons in Fig.18 inherit that bias. Unclear.
4. **Is the elicitation or the model being measured?** Predictability uses a single post-hoc 0–100 self-rating (with an error-count heuristic on parse failure). Defensible as "what API users can do", but then discrimination trends confound model self-knowledge with prompt-format compliance; τ-bench's below-chance AUROCs could partly be elicitation artifacts. The paper cannot distinguish these.
5. **What licenses the aggregate R?** With RPred=Brier, uniform weights, and safety carved out, R is a design choice presented alongside a "science" framing; no sensitivity analysis of conclusions to aggregation is reported. The per-dimension results (Fig.8) are the trustworthy layer; treat R and Fig.1 as illustration.

Cout measures determinism, not quality, so it must always be read jointly with accuracy — which is exactly why the authors keep reliability metrics separate from, not a replacement for, capability metrics." />

## Glossary
- <Term def="probability that all k independent runs of a task succeed, versus pass@k which needs only one; the gap between them is outcome inconsistency">pass∧k</Term>
- <Term def="Expected Calibration Error: confidence-binned mean absolute gap between stated confidence and empirical accuracy; Pcal = 1 − ECE">ECE</Term>
- <Term def="probability that a randomly chosen success received higher self-confidence than a randomly chosen failure; 0.5 = chance">AUROC (discrimination)</Term>
- <Term def="proper scoring rule combining calibration and discrimination: mean squared error between confidence and the 0/1 outcome; reported as 1 − Brier">Brier score</Term>
- <Term def="symmetrized, bounded divergence between two probability distributions; used here between action-type distributions of two runs">Jensen-Shannon divergence</Term>
- <Term def="minimum number of edits turning one action sequence into another, normalized by the longer length; used for sequential trajectory consistency">Levenshtein distance</Term>
- <Term def="Holistic Agent Leaderboard: Princeton's standardized agent-evaluation harness with cost tracking and trace logging; this paper's execution infrastructure">HAL</Term>
- <Term def="scaffold pattern interleaving reasoning text with tool actions and observations; used for the GAIA agent">ReAct</Term>
- <Term def="abstaining on low-confidence predictions to raise accuracy on the retained subset; accuracy-coverage curves visualize it">selective prediction</Term>
