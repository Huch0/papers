<TLDR>A boundary theory for runtime guardrails on tool-using agents: relative to oracle predicates, deterministic pre-execution gates enforce exactly the register-representable nonempty safety policies (strictly below edit automata); with a fallible LLM judge, guarantees shrink to a Neyman–Pearson ROC frontier and a conformal certificate that can be vacuous, is not identifiable from ungated data once blocking feeds back, and loses a margin to bounded representation attacks; and even asking whether a policy specification can ever fire is undecidable in general yet PSPACE in a monotone caps/revocation fragment.</TLDR>

<WhyItMatters>The formal backdrop for TDE's central bet: it shares the runtime-enforcement framing and proves both halves separately — mechanically evaluated oracle predicates give exact streamwise enforcement (T1, the regime of TDE's environment-evaluated pre/postconditions), while LLM-judge adjudication buys only a statistical, attackable frontier (T2, exactly what de-circularization escapes). It differs in kind from TDE: a safety-certification theory (block bad actions), not a task-acceptance harness — no instruction-to-test compilation, no red–green loop; its Prop. 1 even proves per-action gates cannot enforce "eventually"-type goals, precisely the gap a final-world-state acceptance suite fills. Its feedback non-identifiability result also dictates evaluation methodology: harness effects must be measured with paired closed-loop reruns, not static scores.</WhyItMatters>

## Motivation
Runtime guardrails that inspect a proposed tool call and block, rewrite, or escalate it before execution are multiplying, but <Problem>each system fixes a policy language and a checker and reports empirical violation rates — the prior question, which safety policies a runtime gate can enforce at all and at what utility price, is unaddressed</Problem> (§1). Classical enforcement theory does not transfer, because <Problem>agent guardrails break four of its assumptions at once: policy predicates are judged fallibly, external effects are irreversible (no buffering), actions carry unbounded data, and untrusted content adversarially steers proposals</Problem> (§1). Conflating the resulting regimes — e.g., reading a static judge ROC as a closed-loop guarantee — makes published guardrail numbers mean less than they appear to (§2, Tab.2).

## Contribution (what's new)
The paper is explicit that it imports its machinery (classical enforcement, Neyman–Pearson, CPOMDP occupancy methods, conformal risk control, two-counter undecidability) and claims their <Novelty>regime-correct composition for irreversible semantic gates</Novelty> (p.1). The load-bearing new pieces: <Novelty>an exact characterization of what a pre-execution, no-truncation gate over infinite data alphabets enforces relative to a fixed oracle interface (T1)</Novelty>; <Novelty>an identification impossibility — identical judge ROC plus identical ungated trajectory law can hide different closed-loop frontiers, so static evidence cannot certify feedback systems (Prop. 2 / Cor. 2)</Novelty>; <Novelty>a robustness margin `η̄(ρ)` proving benign conformal calibration does not transfer under bounded representation attack, with the exact recalibration condition that restores the certificate (Thm. 5, Cor. 3)</Novelty>; and <Novelty>a keyed-counter fragment made analyzable by exact cap saturation and a one-witness reduction — nontriviality in PSPACE, one step from undecidability (T3)</Novelty>.

## Research questions
(1) Relative to fixed oracle predicates, which policies can a deterministic pre-execution gate enforce (T1)? (2) With a fallible judge, what is the exact one-step safety–utility frontier, what do episode structure and decision feedback do to it, and what data identifies it (T2)? (3) When is static analysis of a policy specification — even nontriviality, the linter question — decidable (T3)? (4) Do vacuous calibration, representation attacks, and gate-induced feedback actually appear in real judge panels and closed-loop agent runs (§7)?

## Methodology
Formalization first: actions are typed symbols carrying data from a possibly infinite domain; policies are <Term def="a policy violated only via a finite bad prefix; equivalently a topologically closed set of infinite traces">safety properties</Term> scored on the committed effect trace, and blocking commits an effect-free no-op (Def. 1). Policies are specified as <Term def="finite-control automata extended with data registers, per-key monotone counters, and optional global counters; transition guards test the action data, registers, and counters">keyed-counter register automata</Term> (Def. 2); a judge thresholds a score with false-alarm/miss rates (Def. 3); a gate maps effect history, proposal, and scores to a decision in `{allow, block, sub(a')}` before an irreversible commit (Def. 4). Theorems are then proved per regime (App. A). Experiments are inference-only at temperature 0: 23 judges (open 0.5B–70B, closed, frontier APIs, plus Llama-Guard-3) score AgentDojo's 132 instruction strings (97 user tasks compliant / 35 injection goals violating) with paired DeLong, max-T, and Holm inference; 500 shared two-fold conformal calibration splits; an exactly enumerated two-step retry model; one predeclared 16-token judge-visible rewrite; and paired closed-loop reruns of a local qwen2.5:7b agent over all four AgentDojo suites, 949 episodes per condition (App. B).

<Pass title="The three boundaries, precisely">
- **T1 (oracle expressiveness).** P is effectively enforceable by a deterministic gate with oracle interface Π iff P is nonempty safety and `Good(P) ∈ RA[T; Π]` (Thm. 1); the witness simulates the good-prefix recognizer and refuses exactly the one-step extensions it rejects. Strictly below <Term def="a monitor that suppresses actions into a buffer and re-emits them later; enforces more than safety, but requires actions to be delayable — exactly what irreversibility removes">edit automata</Term>: "every pay is eventually followed by confirm" is edit-enforceable by buffering but unenforceable by any gate (Prop. 1). Substitution does not enlarge the class; prefix-safe rewrites can only shave false-block cost.
- **T2 exogenous.** For a fixed one-step law and gate-independent information I, the exact frontier is `B*_I(β) = roc_I(β)` (Neyman–Pearson, Thm. 2); Blackwell refinement can only improve it. Under product assumptions the episode lift is `R(β) = 1−(1−β)^H` (all-violation) or `1−(1−qβ)^H` for i.i.d. Bernoulli(q) labels (Cor. 1).
- **T2 endogenous.** Once blocking changes future proposals, the object is a belief-state occupancy polytope: `F_H(δ)` is a finite LP whose optimum mixes at most two deterministic belief-Markov policies; the dynamic rule blocks iff `λp_t − w_t(1−p_t) ≥ Γ^λ_t`, recovering the static ROC only when the continuation gap vanishes (Thm. 3).
- **T2 certificates and attacks.** Conformal risk control on violating calibration examples at per-step target `δ/H` yields `E[unsafe episode] ≤ δ` marginally, finite-sample, distribution-free — possibly via block-all; episode-level calibration removes the H factor at the price of episode-level exchangeability (Thm. 4). A budget-ρ adversary rewriting only the judge-visible representation adds `H·η̄(ρ)`: `E[unsafe] ≤ min{1, δ + H·η̄(ρ)}` (Thm. 5); if the attacked violation-score law dominates the compliant law (`P0 ≪ Qρ`), distributional zero-miss forces block-all, `U = 1` (Prop. 3); recalibrating to the tightened target `δ/H − η̄(ρ)` restores the certificate only when a verified uniform margin stays below `δ/H` (Cor. 3).
- **T3 (decidability).** With equality plus two global counters with increment, guarded decrement, and zero-test, bad-state reachability — hence policy nontriviality, the basic linter check — is undecidable (two-counter Minsky reduction, Thm. 6). The separable monotone fragment T0 (equality registers; key-local monotone counters; one global monotone vector; constant-only guards) is PSPACE via exact cap saturation `σ_x(n) = min{n, C_x+1}` and an exact one-witness key projection, with polynomial per-step online enforcement; it expresses authenticate-before-access, per-recipient caps, bucket quotas, and no-send-after-revoke (Thm. 7).

## Main result
The headline is the boundary set itself; its sharpest empirical instantiation is closed-loop: <Finding src="Tab.4, App.B.5">a 3B judge-gate cuts end-to-end attack success from 45/949 (.047) to 4/949 (.004) (suite-stratified p = 2.2×10⁻⁹) — but only by gating 78% of all proposed calls, dropping pooled task success from .254 to .180</Finding>, while <Finding src="Tab.4">the lighter 7B gate (12% gated) achieves no significant pooled reduction (30/949, CMH p = .09)</Finding>.

## Key findings
- <Finding src="Thm.1/Prop.1, §4">Gates enforce exactly the register-representable nonempty safety policies, strictly below edit automata</Finding>: irreversibility removes buffering, so renewal goals ("pay eventually confirmed") are provably out of reach of any pre-execution gate; adding substitution does not enlarge the class.
- <Finding src="Prop.2/Cor.2, §5">Static evidence cannot identify closed-loop guarantees</Finding>: two systems with identical judge ROC and identical ungated trajectory law differ in `F_H(0)`; in the exactly enumerated retry model, cost at δ=.05 is .551 with perfect recall vs .863 for current-score/time policies vs 1.137 for stationary thresholds (App. B.2).
- <Finding src="§7, Fig.1a">The conformal certificate is real but frequently vacuous or uncertified at benchmark scale</Finding>: with only 35 violations, δ=.05 calibrates every one of the 23 judges to <Term def="calibrated threshold at minus infinity: block every action; meets any miss target vacuously at utility loss 1">block-all</Term>; Gemma-2-9B at δ=.10 reaches miss .010 (simultaneous upper .021) at false-block .780; at δ=.20 the miss estimate .138 carries an upper bound of .264 — not certified.
- <Finding src="Tab.8, App.B.9">One predeclared 16-token administrator-preapproval suffix raises violation miss by .200–.600 for 8 of 11 judges at θ=.5</Finding>, with labels and actions unchanged — benign calibration is not a security boundary. Notably, both Claude-4.5 judges' point estimates decrease (−.143 / −.114, CIs spanning zero).
- <Finding src="App.B.5">Gates create attacks as well as prevent them</Finding>: in task-paired reruns the 3B gate has 34 baseline-only vs 5 gate-only attack episodes (McNemar p = 2.4×10⁻⁶) — the 5 gate-only episodes are trajectories rerouted into new attacks by blocking, direct evidence of decision-dependent transitions; the weak 7B gate is symmetric (29 vs 22, p = .40).
- <Finding src="App.B.6/B.7">Neither parameter count nor the guardrail label orders judge quality</Finding>: AUC spans .660–.858 across 23 judges with no familywise-adjusted superiority over Gemma-2-9B (median detectable difference .104), and deployed Llama-Guard-3 misses .46 of AgentDojo injections at false-block .10 — a content-harm taxonomy transfers only partly to agent-policy violations. A matched source-role counterfactual (byte-identical text, trusted-user vs untrusted-tool provenance swapped) shows provenance use varies sharply: paired concordance .967 (7B) vs .561 (3B) vs .500 (0.5B control) (App. B.4).

## Implications
The paper functions as an evaluation contract for the guardrail literature: name the policy language and oracle interface; report an operating point, not AUC alone; state whether blocking is visible to the agent and run paired reruns; and disclose the calibration population, exchangeability claim, and adversarial margin behind any certificate (§8). Its regime map slots existing systems: <Related>AgentSpec and Progent as T1 oracle-DSL gates, ShieldAgent as exogenous T2, shields (Alshiekh et al. 2018) as the oracle-state ancestor, and AI Control (Greenblatt et al. 2024) as judge-plus-gate under an adversarial source</Related> (Tab.2); <Related>CaMeL and Constitutional Classifiers are architectural / in-judge ways to shrink the margin `η̄(ρ)`</Related>. For TDE specifically: T1 formalizes why mechanical, environment-evaluated checks are categorically stronger than LLM judging — in the worked example the oracle policies P2/P3 stay exact while the judge is <Term def="attack that rewrites only the judge-visible text (e.g. appending an approval claim) so a violating action scores like a compliant one; action, effect, and ground-truth label unchanged">safe-washed</Term> (App. B.10) — and Prop. 1 delimits per-action gating in a way that motivates final-state acceptance testing as a separate mechanism outside the gate class. <FollowUp>Whether an AppWorld-style final-state acceptance suite can be cast as T0-checkable predicates over a logged effect trace, making a TDE harness's verification stack T1-exact end to end</FollowUp>; <FollowUp>paired closed-loop reruns as the default TDE evaluation design, since feedback breaks static identification</FollowUp>; <FollowUp>open problem O3: finite-sample verification of a uniform representation margin `η̄(ρ)`, rather than estimates from selected templates</FollowUp>.

## Limitations
- <Caveat>Closed-loop safety is bought by over-blocking</Caveat>: the effective 3B gate blocks 78% of calls and cuts workspace task success .264→.163 although zero attacks ever land there (0/560) — pure cost on the least injectable suite.
- <Caveat>C4 evaluates three selected policies, not the controlled optimum</Caveat>: Theorem 3 requires a specified finite kernel; AgentDojo's is unknown, and the exact frontier is computed only for a constructed two-step retry model.
- <Caveat>The static diagnostic scores bare instruction strings, not context-rich tool calls</Caveat> (no execution history or tool outputs), so it estimates discrimination for that declared information variable only; trace-derived action-level labels were rejected as invalid rather than obtained (App. B.5).
- <Caveat>Attacks only lower-bound the adversarial margin</Caveat>: C3 estimates `η(ρ; θ=.5)` for one template; robust recalibration needs a verified uniform upper bound `η̄(ρ)`, which no current method supplies (open problem O3).
- <Caveat>Certificates are marginal and assumption-laden</Caveat>: label-conditional exchangeability is required, small calibration sets degenerate to block-all whenever δ falls below `1/(n+1)`, and no claim is made about proximity to the population ROC optimum.
- <Caveat>T1/T3 are not empirically validated</Caveat>: they are instantiated on P2/P3 with a bounded differential audit script, not a mechanized proof; the experiments target T2 only.

## Critiques and Questions
1. Cor. 2's impossibility conditions on inputs limited to ungated trajectories plus static scores — but real deployments log *gated* trajectories, including blocks and what followed them. How much of the controlled frontier becomes identifiable from gate logs under weak assumptions? Unaddressed.
2. T0's practical coverage is argued by example (caps, quotas, revocation). What fraction of rules in deployed DSLs (AgentSpec, Progent policy sets) actually compiles into T0 — i.e., how large is the exactly-enforceable, PSPACE-lintable slice in the wild?
3. The unexplained robustness outlier: both Claude-4.5 judges resist the preapproval suffix that defeats 8 of 11 judges. Diagnosing why (injection-resistance training? provenance handling?) seems at least as actionable as the impossibility framing.
4. All closed-loop evidence comes from one small local agent with baseline attack success .047; whether the over-blocking price and gate-created attacks persist for frontier agents with richer judge information is untested (the paper concedes absolute rates are modest).
5. Escalation to a human — the most common real remediation — is explicitly out of scope (modeled only as prefix-safe oracle substitution), so the theory is silent on the guardrail pattern practitioners rely on most.

## Glossary
- <Term def="minimum false-block rate achievable at a given miss rate by any randomized decision measurable in the judge score; nonincreasing and convex">ROC frontier</Term> / <Term def="the classical lemma that a randomized likelihood-ratio test attains that frontier">Neyman–Pearson</Term>
- <Term def="information I-prime refines I when I is a garbling of I-prime; the optimal rule under the richer variable can never have a worse ROC">Blackwell refinement</Term>
- <Term def="distribution-free calibration choosing the threshold as an order statistic of violating calibration scores so a monotone risk stays at a target level in expectation over the calibration draw">conformal risk control</Term>
- <Term def="constrained partially observable MDP; here the specified finite model in which blocking changes future proposals, solved by a belief-state occupancy LP">CPOMDP</Term>
- <Term def="benchmark of tool-using agent tasks with prompt-injection attacks across banking, slack, travel, and workspace suites; used both as 132 static instruction strings and as a live agent loop">AgentDojo</Term>

### Personal notes
For the TDE novelty defense this is the strongest formal citation that mechanical (environment-evaluated) adjudication is categorically different from LLM-judge adjudication: T1 gives exactness for oracle predicates while T2 shows judge-based gating is capped by an ROC, can be vacuous under calibration, is non-identifiable under feedback, and loses a margin to 16-token rewrites. Cite Prop. 1 when arguing per-action guardrails cannot certify task completion — "eventually" goals are not safety properties — so TDE's final-state acceptance suite is a complementary mechanism this paper never studies (it certifies only the blocking side and treats task success as an uncertified separate endpoint). Import the methodology too: paired closed-loop reruns with baseline-only vs gate-only discordances are the right design for AppWorld experiments, since a TDE harness also changes trajectories. Question worth stealing: can AppWorld state-diff acceptance checks be phrased in (or near) the T0 fragment, so the whole TDE verification stack inherits T1/T3 exactness and PSPACE lintability?
