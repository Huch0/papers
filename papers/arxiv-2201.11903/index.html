Writing worked step-by-step rationales into few-shot exemplars — chain-of-thought prompting — makes large language models generate their own intermediate reasoning before answering, an ability that emerges only around ~100B parameters and that lets PaLM 540B beat the finetuned-GPT-3-plus-verifier SOTA on GSM8K with eight exemplars and zero gradient updates.

The single most influential prompting paper: it created the “reasoning via generated intermediate tokens” paradigm that everything from self-consistency and zero-shot CoT to agent scaffolds and test-time-compute models descends from, and it supplied the canonical evidence for emergent abilities of scale.

Motivation

Two known routes to machine reasoning each had a disqualifying flaw. Training or finetuning models to produce natural-language rationales (Ling et al. 2017; Cobbe et al. 2021) works, but writing thousands of high-quality rationales per task is far costlier than collecting plain input-output pairs. And few-shot prompting à la GPT-3 needs no data, but performs badly on reasoning tasks and — worse — stays flat as models scale (§1). The field’s takeaway circa 2021 (e.g., the Gopher report) was that arithmetic, commonsense, and symbolic reasoning are things scaling alone does not buy.

Contribution (what’s new)

Merge the two routes so each cancels the other’s flaw: put the rationale inside the prompt — few-shot exemplars become input → chain of thought → answer triples, so an off-the-shelf model imitates the format and generates its own reasoning steps before its answer, at a cost of eight hand-written examples instead of a training set (§2). Two claims elevate this above a prompting trick: the ability is emergent — CoT hurts models below ~10B (they produce fluent but illogical chains) and only pays off around 100B, turning previously-flat scaling curves steeply upward (§3.2), and the ablations show the design is minimal: neither extra computation (dots), nor equations alone, nor reasoning given after the answer reproduces the gain — the load-bearing element is natural-language intermediate steps generated before the answer (§3.3).

Research questions

  1. Can few-shot prompting alone elicit multi-step reasoning that finetuned, task-specific systems needed verifiers and architectures for? (§1)
  2. Is the effect specific to arithmetic, or general across commonsense and symbolic reasoning? (§4–5)
  3. Why does it work — extra compute, equation extraction, knowledge activation, or the generated reasoning itself? (§3.3)
  4. How sensitive is it to who writes the exemplars, which exemplars, their order and number? (§3.4, App.A.2)

Methodology

Take the standard 8-shot prompt and rewrite each exemplar’s answer as a short worked solution ending in “The answer is X.” (Fig.1: Roger’s tennis balls — “Roger started with 5 balls. 2 cans of 3 tennis balls each is 6 tennis balls. 5 + 6 = 11.”). One fixed exemplar set, written by one author without prompt engineering, is reused across all math benchmarks (AQuA gets 4 exemplars from its train set); greedy decoding; only the final answer is scored (§3.1).

Evaluation grid and variants
  • Models: GPT-3 (350M–175B, text-ada through text-davinci-002), LaMDA (422M–137B), PaLM (8B–540B), UL2 20B, Codex (code-davinci-002). No model is finetuned. LaMDA results average 5 random exemplar orders; other models one order (§3.1).
  • Tasks: arithmetic — GSM8K, SVAMP, ASDiv, AQuA, MAWPS (Tab.12); commonsense — CSQA, StrategyQA, BIG-bench Date & Sports Understanding, SayCan robot planning (§4); symbolic — last-letter concatenation and coin-flip state tracking, each with in-domain (2 steps, matching exemplars) and out-of-domain longer variants (§5).
  • External calculator (optional post-hoc): run every generated equation through Python eval, propagating results between equations — worth at most a few points (Tab.1).
  • Ablations (§3.3): equation only (emit the equation, then answer); variable compute only (emit dots matching the equation’s character count); chain of thought after answer.

Main result

With 8 exemplars and no training, PaLM 540B + CoT reaches 56.9% on GSM8K (58.6% with calculator) versus 17.9% for standard prompting — surpassing the prior SOTA of 55% from finetuned GPT-3 175B with a trained verifier (Tab.1, Fig.2). CoT sets new SOTA on GSM8K, SVAMP, and MAWPS and comes within 2% on ASDiv and AQuA (§3.2).

Key findings

  • CoT is an emergent ability: below ~10B parameters it hurts (GPT-3 6.7B GSM8K: 4.0 → 2.4; LaMDA 420M: 2.6 → 0.4) because small models produce fluent but illogical chains; gains appear only near 100B (Tab.2, §3.2) — the definitional example for the emergent-abilities literature.
  • Gains concentrate where problems are multi-step and the scaling curve is flat: MultiArith jumps 42.2 → 94.7 while one-step SingleOp stays at 94.1 → 94.1 for PaLM 540B (Tab.3, App.A.3) — CoT buys reasoning depth, not knowledge.
  • All three ablations collapse to the baseline on GSM8K (equation-only 5.4, dots 6.4, reasoning-after-answer 6.1, vs CoT 14.3 for LaMDA 137B) (Fig.5, Tab.6): the benefit is not extra token compute nor knowledge activation — the model depends on its generated natural-language steps to reach the answer.
  • The gap survives different annotators (14.3/15.5/17.6), a concise style (11.1), exemplars sampled from GSM8K’s own train set (12.6–12.7), varying exemplar order and count — all far above standard’s 6.5 (Tab.6–7, §3.4); but variance is real: on coin flip, annotator prompts range 71.4–99.6, and for a list-reversal task two co-authors failed to write any working CoT prompt while a third succeeded (App.A.2).
  • Error anatomy: of LaMDA 137B’s wrong GSM8K answers, 46% are near-misses (8% pure calculator slips, 16% symbol-mapping errors, 22% one missing step) and 54% have genuine semantic/coherence failures; scaling PaLM 62B → 540B fixes 12/18 one-step-missing and 6/20 semantic-understanding errors (App.D.2, Fig.9) — scale buys semantic understanding, the calculator patches arithmetic.
  • CoT enables length generalization: on out-of-domain 4-word letter concatenation, standard prompting scores 0.0 and CoT reaches 63.0 (coin flip 4-flip: 54.8 → 90.2) (Tab.5, §5) — the model reuses the demonstrated procedure on longer inputs than it ever saw.
  • When the final answer is right, the reasoning usually is too: of 50 correct LaMDA GSM8K chains, essentially all were logically and mathematically sound (1–2 coincidentally correct) (App.D.1) — though for multiple-choice/binary tasks, right-for-wrong-reasons is much more likely (App.D.1).
  • Transfer across models is imperfect: the same CoT prompts hurt GPT-3 on CSQA (79.5 → 73.5) and StrategyQA (Tab.4, App.A.2) — prompt-model interaction is unexplained.

Implications

This paper converted “reasoning” from a finetuning problem into a decoding-time problem, spawning self-consistency (majority vote over sampled chains, cited as follow-up in §3.1), zero-shot CoT, STaR-style bootstrapped rationale training, and ultimately the test-time-compute / reasoning-model line; the SayCan result seeds CoT-based planning in embodied and software agents. Its framing that “standard prompting only provides a lower bound on model capabilities” (§6) reshaped how the field evaluates LLMs, and its emergence claim became the centerpiece of the emergent-abilities debate (Wei et al., 2022b). Chase next: whether emergence is a property of the model or of exact-match metrics (the “mirage” critique), eliciting reasoning below 100B (distillation, rationale finetuning), and faithfulness — whether the stated chain is the computation that produced the answer.

Limitations

  • The paper explicitly does not claim the model is “reasoning” — whether the chains reflect actual computation is left open (§6).
  • No correctness guarantee: chains can be wrong yet yield right answers and vice versa; factuality of generations is unaddressed (§6, App.D.2).
  • Only helps at ~100B+ scale, making it expensive to serve, and “model scale is conflated with other factors, such as amount of training compute” (§6, App.A.1) — the emergence threshold is not causally isolated.
  • Manual rationale cost is trivial for 8-shot prompting but prohibitive if the same idea were applied as finetuning data (§6).
  • Robustness statistics (5 seeds, std devs) exist only for LaMDA; GPT-3/PaLM results are single runs of a single exemplar order, justified by cost (Tab.6 caption).

Critiques and Questions

  1. Is the sharp emergence real or metric-induced? Solve-rate on exact final answers can turn a smoothly improving underlying capability into an apparent phase transition (a multi-step answer is all-or-nothing). The paper’s own hedge — scale conflated with compute and data (App.A.1) — plus the later “mirage” literature make the ~100B threshold the paper’s most contestable claim.
  2. Internal inconsistency in the faithfulness analysis: §3.2 says 2 of 50 correct chains were coincidentally correct; App.D.1 says only 1 — and the acknowledgements thank a reader for catching a mistake in the original error analysis, suggesting this audit was fragile.
  3. Contamination is untested here. GSM8K and its rationales were public before these models’ training cutoffs; this paper does no n-gram overlap analysis (the PaLM paper’s separate analysis found none for its corpus, but GPT-3/Codex remain unaudited).
  4. The prior-SOTA comparisons pit a 540B prompted model against ≤175B finetuned models from a year earlier — fair as a “prompting can match finetuning” claim, less so as a systems comparison.
  5. Why the same prompts hurt GPT-3 on CSQA/StrategyQA is never explained — a crack in the “general method” framing that later prompt-sensitivity work widened.
What do the three ablations (equation-only, dots-only, reasoning-after-answer) each rule out as the explanation for CoT's gains?
Equation-only (5.4 on GSM8K vs CoT 14.3) rules out 'it just extracts the math': GSM8K semantics are too hard to translate directly to an equation. Variable-compute-only dots (6.4 ≈ baseline 6.5) rules out 'more tokens = more computation' by itself. Reasoning-after-answer (6.1 ≈ baseline) rules out 'the prompt merely activates relevant pretraining knowledge' — the answer must be conditioned on the generated steps. Together they show natural-language intermediate reasoning produced before the answer is the operative ingredient (Fig.5, Tab.6).
