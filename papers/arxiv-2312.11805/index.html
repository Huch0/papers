Gemini 1.0 is a family of natively multimodal Transformer decoders (Ultra / Pro / Nano) trained jointly on text, image, audio, and video from the start, reaching state of the art on 30 of 32 reported benchmarks — including the first score above human-expert level on MMLU (90.04%) — while the report doubles as a systems paper on training stably across TPU datacenters at 97% goodput.

This is the milestone where “one model, all modalities, from scratch” replaced the graft-a-vision-encoder recipe as the frontier default, and it set the template — capability-family releases (flagship/serving/on-device), inference-time compute for headline numbers, and infrastructure as a first-class contribution — that Gemini 1.5, GPT-4o, and Llama 3 all follow.

Motivation

Think of a photo of a teacher’s hand-drawn skier problem with a student’s messy handwritten solution (Fig.1): grading it requires reading bad handwriting, parsing a physics setup, and doing math — perception and reasoning in one pass. Prior frontier systems were text models with vision grafted on afterwards (Flamingo, PaLI, CoCa bolt encoders onto a language model), so cross-modal reasoning is bottlenecked by the seam between components. The open question we set out to answer (§5): can a single model trained jointly on all modalities match models narrowly tailored to each single domain — or does joint training tax every domain? A second, less glamorous problem forced itself on us: at a scale well beyond PaLM-2, hardware fails constantly and Silent Data Corruption strikes every week or two, so the conventional checkpoint-to-disk training loop wastes a large fraction of the fleet (§3).

Contribution (what’s new)

Native multimodality: one decoder trained on interleaved text, image, audio, and video from the beginning — with discrete image tokens so it can also output images — and the empirical answer that this generalist is simultaneously state of the art in each modality (§2, §5). The design is forced by the failure mode of retrofits: a pipeline that captions audio or images into text loses exactly the fine-grained signal (16kHz audio nuance, pixel-level layout) the hard tasks need. Two supporting deltas matter on their own: uncertainty-routed chain-of-thought — sample k=32 CoT chains, take the majority answer only when consensus clears a validated threshold, otherwise fall back to greedy (App 10.2), and replacing periodic disk checkpoints with redundant in-memory model-state replicas, recovering from intact replicas on failure, which lifted goodput from 85% (PaLM/PaLM-2) to 97% (§3).

Research questions

  1. Does joint multimodal training produce a model that is state of the art per domain, not just on cross-modal tasks? (§5)
  2. What infrastructure makes synchronous training stable across multiple datacenters of TPUv4 SuperPods? (§3)
  3. How do capabilities distribute across a size family — what survives distillation into a 1.8B–3.25B on-device model? (§5.1.3)

Methodology

Gemini 1.0 models are decoder-only Transformers with efficiency modifications (e.g. multi-query attention), trained at 32K context (§2). Inputs are interleaved sequences: video is fed as frames in the long context, audio enters as 16kHz USM features, and images can be produced natively via discrete image tokens. Three sizes (Tab.1): Ultra (flagship), Pro (cost/latency-optimized, pre-trained in weeks on a fraction of Ultra’s resources), Nano-1/Nano-2 (1.8B/3.25B, distilled from larger Gemini models, 4-bit quantized). Token budget for Ultra follows Chinchilla scaling (Hoffmann et al. 2022); the smaller models are deliberately over-trained on more tokens for inference efficiency, as advocated by LLaMA (§4). Post-training (§6) is the now-standard pipeline — curated prompts, SFT on demonstrations, reward-model training on human preferences, then RLHF — producing two variants: Gemini Apps (the Gemini/Bard product) and Gemini API (AI Studio / Vertex).

Method details: infrastructure, data, post-training

Infrastructure (§3). TPUv4 is deployed in SuperPods of 4096 chips whose optical switches reconfigure 4x4x4 cubes into arbitrary 3D-torus topologies in about 10 seconds; we keep a few cubes per SuperPod as hot standbys and for rolling maintenance. Ultra trains synchronously across datacenters: model parallelism within SuperPods, data parallelism across them, orchestrated by a single Python process (JAX + Pathways single-controller model), with GSPMD partitioning and MegaScale XLA static collective scheduling. Goodput reached 97% because recovery pulls model state from an intact in-memory replica instead of persistent storage. Silent Data Corruption events, expected every week or two at this scale, are caught with deterministic replay to isolate incorrect computations plus proactive SDC scanners on idle machines — full determinism was a crucial ingredient of stable training.

Data (§4). Web documents, books, code, plus image/audio/video; SentencePiece tokenizer trained on a large corpus sample (better non-Latin tokenization); quality + safety filtering; evaluation-set decontamination; mixture weights set by ablations on small models, with staged training that up-weights domain-relevant data near the end. Data quality mattered more than any other dataset lever we report.

Post-training capabilities (§6.5). Instruction-following data includes synthetically verified constraints (e.g. word counts). Tool use is framed as code generation: every tool invocation is a code block, so the model composes multiple tools and reacts to execution results in a sampling-execution loop (Fig.8). Multilingual coverage comes from localized (not merely translated) English data with translatability filtering. Safety mitigation targets ~20 enumerated harm types with SFT data partly generated by a Constitutional-AI-style recipe injecting policy text as constitutions, plus safety-specific RLHF preference data (§7.3.2).

Main result

Gemini Ultra tops GPT-4 on 10 of 12 text/reasoning benchmarks and is the first model past the human-expert threshold on MMLU: 90.04% (uncertainty-routed CoT@32) vs 89.8% expert accuracy and 86.4% prior SOTA (Tab.2, p.8); on multimodal benchmarks it leads GPT-4V everywhere reported, e.g. MMMU 62.4% vs 56.8% — using pixels only, no external OCR (Tab.7-8, p.13-14).

Key findings

  • The MMLU headline is an inference-procedure result as much as a model result (App 10.2, Fig.9): greedy decoding gives Ultra 84.0% (GPT-4: 84.2% — a tie), plain CoT@32 only 85.0%, and uncertainty-routed CoT@32 90.04%. GPT-4 gains nothing from the routing (87.3% either way). The routing works precisely when a model’s CoT samples are inconsistent — so Ultra’s chains are strong but noisy.
  • Benchmark scores are alarmingly sensitive to pretraining-data composition (§5.1.1, p.7): a mere hundred fine-tuning steps on HellaSwag-adjacent website extracts pushed Ultra from 87.8% to 96.0% (1-shot). This is why we dropped LAMBADA outright and built held-out sets (Natural2Code, WMT23, Math-AMC 2022-23) — on which margins stay positive but shrink (Natural2Code 74.9 vs 73.9; AMC 32% vs 30%).
  • Goodput 85% → 97% came from in-memory model-state redundancy plus deterministic replay for SDC isolation (§3, p.5) — at this fleet size, machine failure is the steady state and checkpoint-restore from disk is the binding constraint, not FLOPs.
  • Post-training gains concentrate exactly where targeted (Tab.6, Tab.15): factuality inaccuracy halved (6.7% → 3.8%), attribution AIS 40.2% → 60.0%, hedging accuracy 0% → 69.3%; tool-use fine-tuning lifts RealtimeQA from 39.2% to 70.8% and GSM8K from 69.7% to 80.1%. Meanwhile vision SFT adds only +0.8 to +3.3 points over the pre-trained model (Tab.17) — the raw capability was already in pre-training; post-training mostly aligns output format.
  • Per-instruction accuracy (87.4%, Gemini Advanced) far exceeds full-response accuracy (54.1%) (Tab.14, p.22): failures are spread thinly across responses, so nearly half of complex prompts still miss at least one sub-instruction.
  • Capabilities compress unevenly into Nano (Tab.3, Fig.3): at 1.8B/3.25B, factuality/retrieval retains 0.69–0.91 of Pro’s score but reasoning collapses (BIG-Bench-Hard 0.47/0.58, closed-book NQ 0.43/0.56) — distillation preserves knowledge-lookup far better than multi-step reasoning.
  • The 32K context is genuinely used (§5.1.5, Fig.4): 98% synthetic key-value retrieval accuracy across the full window and monotonically decreasing NLL up to position 32K.

Implications

The report’s answer — joint training costs you nothing per-domain and buys cross-modal reasoning — made natively multimodal training the frontier default (GPT-4o, Gemini 1.5), and its family structure (flagship / serveable / on-device distilled) became the standard release shape. AlphaCode 2 (§5.1.7) previews model+search agents: a tuned Pro doing massive sampling with filtering/clustering/reranking solves 43% of Codeforces problems (85th percentile) vs AlphaCode’s 25% — the composition of a strong base model with search foreshadows inference-time-compute scaling, as does uncertainty-routed CoT itself. How the 32K context scaled to 1M+ in Gemini 1.5 (and whether MoE was involved — this report is silent on architecture beyond the basics), whether uncertainty-routed self-consistency survives as a general technique or was benchmark-specific, and the contamination methodology debate this report’s HellaSwag experiment fueled are the threads to chase.

Limitations

  • Parameter counts (except Nano), data scale, and training compute are all undisclosed — the model card literally lists Compute Requirements and Model Stats as Not reported (App 10.1), so nothing here is reproducible or even placeable on a scaling curve.
  • Ultra was never evaluated on audio (§5.2.4); speech numbers are Pro/Nano-1 only. Video understanding means 16 uniformly sampled frames (§5.2.2), not dense temporal modeling.
  • Author-stated (§8): hallucinations persist, and the models still struggle with causal understanding, logical deduction, and counterfactual reasoning despite exam-benchmark saturation.
  • Image generation is demonstrated only qualitatively (Fig.6, Fig.12) — zero quantitative evaluation.
  • Dangerous-capability testing found early checkpoints vulnerable to simple jailbreaks and prompt injection (§7.4.3.1), and CTF results show the model solves entry-level challenges but fails long-range planning (§7.4.1.3) — safety evidence is preliminary and largely internal.

Critiques and Questions

  1. The comparison table mixes inference procedures per cell: MMLU CoT@32 (uncertainty-routed, threshold tuned on the validation split) vs GPT-4’s 5-shot reported number; HumanEval and WMT23 use post-trained Gemini but reported/API numbers for rivals, self-collected in Nov 2023. At matched greedy decoding, Ultra and GPT-4 are statistically indistinguishable on MMLU (84.0 vs 84.2) — “first past human expert” is really “first with this much inference-time compute and routing”.
  2. “SOTA on 30 of 32” is over a self-selected suite, and the 9-of-9 image-benchmark claim counts generalist comparisons while fine-tuned specialists still win AI2D (81.4 vs 79.5) and VQAv2 (86.1 vs 77.8) (Tab.7, gray rows).
  3. The HellaSwag contamination experiment is admirably honest but cuts both ways: if 100 steps of web-extract fine-tuning moves a benchmark 8 points, how much of any model’s table — including this one — reflects mixture proximity rather than capability? The report does not quantify residual contamination on the benchmarks it does keep.
  4. The natively-multimodal-beats-retrofit thesis is asserted, not ablated: there is no controlled comparison against an equally-resourced encoder-grafted baseline, so the causal claim rests on cross-lab comparisons with unmatched compute.
  5. Human-preference evals (SxS scores, Tab.16/18) come from internal raters on internal prompt sets with no released rubric — informative for tracking progress, weak as external evidence.
Gemini Ultra reports 90.04% on MMLU while GPT-4 reports 87.29% under CoT@32 — why does this gap mostly vanish under greedy decoding, and what does the uncertainty-routed procedure exploit?
Under greedy decoding Ultra scores 84.0% vs GPT-4's 84.2% (App 10.2). The routed procedure samples 32 CoT chains and accepts the majority answer only when consensus exceeds a validation-tuned threshold, reverting to greedy otherwise. It exploits the fact that Ultra's CoT samples are strong but inconsistent: plain majority voting over noisy chains yields only 85.0%, while routing away the low-consensus cases recovers 90.04%. GPT-4's chains are already self-consistent, so routing adds nothing over plain CoT@32 (87.3% either way).
