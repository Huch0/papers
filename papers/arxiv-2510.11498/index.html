We train a 7B/8B open coding policy with GRPO on a reward from a frozen multimodal LLM scoring three timed screenshots of the rendered page, teach it to call that critic as a tool in a generate–diagnose–refine loop, admit only strictly improving revisions (Forced Optimization), and drop the critic at inference for critic-free self-editing.

The closest published blueprint to an open-weight coder trained with rendering and a visual judge in the loop; its failure modes (behavioral collapse, reflection avoidance, weak multi-file gains) are what a thesis on interactive-component synthesis must design around.

Motivation

Take a prompt from our Fig. 6: “Create a classical chess board in HTML format that can be run directly.” A text-only coder returns code that renders without error, yet the board is over-simplified and misaligned (App. H); no unit test or linter flags that. Front-end correctness lives in rendered pixels and interaction, where one misaligned square is a failure, and a text-only policy is blind to the pixel consequences of its own code: layout drift, interaction breakage, aesthetic inconsistency (§1, §3.7). Prior work closes half the loop: one-shot vision-to-code generates but never refines; self-refinement frameworks refine only on pixel-blind signals (unit tests, linters) (§1). And once a small model gets rich visual feedback we hit behavioral collapse: the revised chessboard is often worse than the draft (§2.6, Fig.4). The problem is not “add a critic” but “make a 7B model improve under a critic without regressing or gaming the reward”.

Contribution (what’s new)

The perceptual judge goes inside the RL loop twice: as reward (a frozen MLLM critic scores the chessboard’s rendered screenshots) and as a tool the policy learns to call mid-trajectory for vision-grounded repair text (§2.2–2.5). Two rules are forced: zero reward for invalid renders, else a code-reading judge over-rewards unrenderable pages (§2.5); Forced Optimization: admit a revision only if it strictly beats the best-so-far, because penalising regressions taught the agent to stop reflecting (§2.6, App. E). Masked critic tokens let the policy internalise reflection and run critic-free (§2.4, §2.7).

Research questions

  1. Does a screenshot-scored MLLM reward beat text-only signals for a small open coder on front-end tasks?
  2. Does calling the critic during rollouts add value over the same reward without reflection (Web-RL)?
  3. Does the learned reflection survive removing the critic at inference, and at what latency?

Methodology

Follow the chessboard through one training rollout. Round 1: the policy writes plan t1 and code c1, then emits <get feedback>. Our sandbox (headless Chromium, no network, seeded timers) renders c1 and screenshots it at post-load, +1s and +2s; the critic reads prompt, code and the three frames in one call and returns a round score plus feedback m1 on what it sees, here the grid alignment (§2.3, §2.5, §3.1). Round 2: given {q, t1, c1, m1}, the policy proposes c2. Under Forced Optimization, c2 enters the trajectory only if it scores strictly higher; otherwise we resample, up to 10 times, then stop and keep c1 (§2.6, Alg.1). Rounds are capped at three (§3.3). This is LLM-as-a-judge as a training reward; the in-rollout call is agent-as-a-verifier as a tool.

Why the rest is forced. A pixel-blind reward cannot see the misaligned grid, so the critic must look at renders (§2.5); a plausible page that never renders would be scored from its code, so invalid renders get zero (§2.5); critic tokens get zero advantage, so the policy is not paid for the critic’s text (§2.4); the training critic is decoupled from the offline judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro) so we do not overfit one judge’s quirks (§1, App. B); and since each critic call needs eight H20 GPUs plus a browser, we drop it at inference and let the policy self-edit for up to three rounds (§2.7, §3.5).

Method details
  1. Rollout format — Output o = t1 ⊕ c1 ⊕ m1 ⊕ … ⊕ tR ⊕ cR (Eq.1); m_r is wrapped in <mllm feedback>. R is capped at 3 reflection rounds in training and inference (§3.3). The template tells the model to emit <get feedback> unless it believes the answer is flawless, and to write executable HTML/CSS/JS/SVG (App. I, Fig.7).
  2. Sandbox — Headless Chromium, filesystem/network off, dangerous APIs blocked (window.open, alert/confirm/prompt, eval/Function, clipboard, non-local fetch/XHR/WebSocket), non-whitelisted origins fail closed, local font/image fixtures, seeded timers and animations, strict CSP (no inline or remote scripts), per-sample wall-clock timeout, full-page screenshots at T=3 time points (§3.1).
  3. Critic call — One scoring call per round over prompt, code and the three frames returns one round-level score on the ArtifactsBench rubric (spec adherence, layout/spatial fidelity, typography/colour coherence, interactive integrity when actions are specified) plus feedback text; no multi-judge averaging or smoothing (§2.5, App. D).
  4. Forced Optimization — Accept (t_{r+1}, c_{r+1}) only if its score strictly exceeds best-so-far: no margin, no re-scoring; at most K=10 resamples per round; if none improves, terminate reflection for that trajectory and keep best-so-far (§2.6, Alg.1, App. G). An earlier negative-reward penalty for regressions was dropped because it made the agent reflect less (App. E).
  5. Optimisation — GRPO with G=8, ε=0.2, KL weight β swept in [0.01, 0.05] (step 0.01), advantage clip swept in {1, 2, 3} with [−2, 2] as default, lr 1e-6, linear warmup 5% then cosine decay, batch 256, at most 40 steps, bf16/fp16 with activation checkpointing, 64 GPUs (32 policy, 32 MLLM), 80 min per step; checkpoints chosen by mean VisualScore (§3.2, App. G).
  6. Inference — Drop screenshots and MLLM calls; the policy runs up to three critic-free self-edit rounds (ReLook-w/o-MLLM). ReLook keeps the critic where accuracy matters; on the unit-test benchmarks both variants use identical critic-free inference (§2.7, Tab.1 caption).

Evaluation: six ArtifactsBench subsets (A-Lite 300 random, A-Easy 305, A-Game 413, A-SVG 123, A-Web 447, A-Si 75) scored by Gemini-2.5-Pro on the official scripts and rubric; FullStack-Bench-Html; and Web-Bench. All numbers are means over three seeds at temperature 1.0, top-p 0.7, with identical prompts and rendering across systems (§3.1–3.2, App. B, App. G).

Method checks: fairness, variance, judge circularity, contamination
  • Same base model and compute for baselines? Base, Web-RL and ReLook share the backbone, prompts, decoding (temp 1.0, top-p 0.7) and rendering infrastructure (§3.2). ReLook trains at most 40 steps, batch 256, G=8 (§3.2); whether Web-RL received the same number of steps and rollouts is not reported. GPT-4o and Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct are untrained reference points under the same protocol (§3.2).
  • Single run or variance? Means over three seeds everywhere, ablations on identical seeds (Tab.1 caption, §3.2, §3.4, App. G). No standard deviation, confidence interval or significance test is reported.
  • Critic reliability vs humans? Inherited from ArtifactsBench: over 90% agreement (Cohen’s κ > 0.85) between Gemini-2.5-Pro / Qwen2.5-VL-72B and expert raters on 200 stratified tasks, three raters, double-blind; Spearman ρ = 0.87 (Gemini) and 0.83 (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) against WebDev Arena’s more than 10,000 pairwise comparisons (App. B). Our own human check is a single GSB vote: 100 tasks, five annotators, majority vote, no agreement statistic (§3.2).
  • Is the evaluation judge the same family as the training critic? No: training critic Qwen2.5-VL-72B-Instruct, offline evaluator Gemini-2.5-Pro, decoupled explicitly to mitigate on-policy judge overfitting (§1, §3.1, App. B). Residual circularity: both judges are validated by the same ArtifactsBench rubric from our own group, Fig.4 is scored by the training judge (Fig.4 caption), and checkpoint selection is by mean VisualScore with the judge unspecified (§3.2).
  • Contamination and aging. Instance-level train–test de-duplication with the lexical/DOM/code thresholds above (§3.1). Contamination of the backbones’ pretraining corpora with the benchmarks: not reported. Aging or versioning of the evaluation sets: not reported; the test subsets are strict subsets of the 1,825 ArtifactsBench tasks (App. B).

Main result

On Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, ReLook lifts A-Lite from 21.59 to 27.88, above Web-RL at 24.89, and the critic-free variant still reaches 25.44 (Tab.1); ReLook > Web-RL > Base holds on every ArtifactsBench subset on both backbones (Fig.2, Fig.3). ReLook-7B passes Qwen2.5-32B-Instruct on A-Lite (25.73), trailing GPT-4o (33.25). The base model’s over-simple chessboard now renders with precise alignment (Fig.6, App. H).

Key findings

  • Every component matters; Forced Optimization is the largest piece (Tab.2, §3.4): on A-Lite, MLLM reward alone 21.59 → 24.89 (+3.3), invalid-render zero reward → 25.84 (+1.0), Forced Optimization → 27.88 (+2.0).
  • Behavioral collapse is real: with the same feedback, Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct degrades after 2–3 reflection rounds while ReLook improves monotonically over eight forced rounds (Fig.4); training-judge scores, not independent.
  • Penalising regressions backfires; a hard acceptance filter does not (§2.6, App. E, Fig.5): the penalty made the agent request feedback less, degrading ReLook into plain RL; under Forced Optimization reflection frequency rises and settles near 2 rounds.
  • Valid Render Rate rises from about 40% at initialisation to about 80% at convergence (§2.5, App. G); the prompt’s “write executable HTML/CSS/JS/SVG” constraint keeps early rewards from being too sparse.
  • Internalised reflection keeps most of the gain at near-base latency (Tab.1, §3.5): w/o-MLLM beats Web-RL on every subset, and per-query time drops from 123.04 s to 18.03 s (100 queries; policy on 4, critic on 8 H20s).
  • Gains concentrate on visual subsets and stay small on multi-file projects (§3.6, Tab.1): Web-Bench 3.00 → 4.20 pass@2 (40% relative), far below GPT-4o’s 23.80; Web-RL lowers FullStack-Html 65.00 → 63.25 while ReLook recovers 67.50, which we do not discuss.

Implications

We read this as: an MLLM judge from the ArtifactsBench lineage can be a training reward, not only an evaluator, and the hard part is trajectory hygiene (monotonic acceptance, invalid-render zeroing), not the judge itself. ReLook sits between one-shot vision-to-code (Design2Code, Web2Code, UICoder, DesignCoder) and tool-critique self-refinement (CRITIC, Reflexion, Self-Refine); Forced Optimization is an online, in-trajectory relative of best-of-N selection (§4.2). Worth chasing: scripted interaction traces instead of passive frames, so the reward sees clicks and form state (model-based GUI testing); a lighter or distilled critic, since 32 of our 64 GPUs serve it (§3.2, App. A); joint unit-test and visual rewards, given Web-RL’s FullStack-Html regression.

Limitations

  • We know our validity chain is borrowed: training critic (Qwen2.5-VL-72B) and offline judge (Gemini-2.5-Pro) are both MLLM judges validated once by ArtifactsBench; we did not re-run human studies beyond a 100-task GSB vote with no agreement statistic, and Fig.4 is scored by the training judge (§1, §3.2, App. A–B).
  • We report three-seed means without variance or significance tests, so sub-point deltas (e.g. Llama Web-RL on A-Lite, 21.04 to 21.67) may be seed noise; we did not report the rejection rate or the γ sweep, so reflection settling near 2 rounds under a cap of 3 (Fig.5) cannot be separated from resample budgets failing, and the acceptance rule may reduce exploration on hard tasks (Tab.1, §3.2, App. A, App. G).
  • “Interaction” is inferred from three passive screenshots, no user action executed; our deterministic sandbox under-represents device and locale variability; and the reward passes through an external evaluator and a length penalty, so metric drift and prompt sensitivity can bias optimisation (§2.5, §3.1, App. A).
  • We trained only 7B/8B backbones, at most 40 steps, front-end only, with a 72B critic on 32 of 64 GPUs; Web-Bench stays low, and code is planned for release upon publication (§3.2, §3.6, App. A).

Critiques and Questions

  1. The §3.6 prose disagrees with Table 1 (deltas computed from Tab.1): “3.2–6.1 points” on visual subsets fits only Qwen A-SVG and A-Game (Qwen A-Web is +2.62; every Llama visual subset is below 3.2); “2.4–3.4 on A-Easy” misses Llama’s +4.75; the largest Qwen gain, A-Si (+7.75), goes unmentioned.
  2. Web-RL vs ReLook conflates in-rollout reflection with Forced Optimization, and Table 2’s middle row (25.84) has no reflection either, so reflection alone is never isolated. And since the acceptance criterion is the reward model, Forced Optimization is rejection sampling against the critic (up to 10 draws per round); the w/o-MLLM transfer suggests some real learning, but the γ ablation is never reported.
  3. On FullStack-Html and Web-Bench, “ReLook” is critic-free inference (Tab.1 caption), so “consistent gains across three benchmarks” rests on w/o-MLLM for two of three; and Web-RL’s FullStack-Html drop (65.00 to 63.25), a visual-only reward trading function for looks, goes undiscussed.
Chessboard revision c2 scores below draft c1: what happens under Forced Optimization, and why not penalise c2?
c2 is rejected without re-scoring and a new revision is sampled, up to 10 times; if none beats c1, reflection stops and c1 is the answer, so accepted trajectories are monotone. A regression penalty was tried first and made the agent stop requesting feedback (fewer reflections, fewer penalties), collapsing ReLook into plain RL; the hard filter blocks regressions without discouraging reflection (§2.6, Alg.1, App. E, Fig.5).
