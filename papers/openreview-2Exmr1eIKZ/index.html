AgentSuite is a unified runner for LLM tool-use/agent benchmarks that ships cleaned and filtered variants of eight-plus benchmark suites, with defective tasks identified by COBA — a taxonomy-grounded, two-stage (benchmark-aware rules, then LLM-as-Judge) auditing pipeline with human verification.

Benchmark auditing is squarely in my evaluation-methodology lane: it audits exactly the harness benchmarks I track (tau-bench, tau2-bench, BFCL v3/v4, ACEBench) and quantifies how much reported agent scores ride on defective tasks.

Motivation

Per the repo’s framing, agent benchmarks contain identifiable task-level defects — e.g. “malformed tools, incorrect ground truth” (README) — spanning “benchmark tasks, tools, evaluation, and environments” (pipeline/README.md), so reported agent scores conflate model failures with benchmark defects, undermining evaluation reliability and cross-benchmark comparability. The paper’s own statement of the gap versus prior benchmark-quality work is unavailable (see caveat above).

Contribution (what’s new)

Two deliverables, per the repo:

  • COBA (Component-Based Benchmark Auditing): a pipeline that “operationalizes a structured taxonomy of benchmark task-level issues and applies a two-stage process combining rule-based detection and LLM-based assessment, with human verification” (src: pipeline/README.md). Stage 1 applies benchmark-aware deterministic rules for “structural or misalignment issues”; Stage 2 uses LLM-as-Judge for “more subtle or semantic issues that are difficult to capture via rules”.
  • AgentSuite: one-touch execution of the audited benchmarks plus cleaned benchmark variants that “mitigate identifiable evaluation issues while preserving comparability with original benchmark settings” (src: README), surfaced as Original-vs-Filtering scores on a public Hugging Face leaderboard.

Methodology

As far as the repo documents it — the paper’s method section is unavailable.

What the audit consumes. Recorded evaluation trajectories, not live runs: per-model JSONL files containing the full message/tool-call trace plus an eval result; “This pipeline does NOT run benchmark evaluations” (src: pipeline/README.md). The default Stage-2 judge is google/gemini-2.5-pro-thinking-on, with an optional rebuttal round (--rebuttal).

What it outputs. Per benchmark: identified issue cases per task, rule-based and LLM-based filtering decisions, “alignment with human-labeled ground truth” (precision/recall against a human_labelled_ground_truth/ set), and retention statistics (src: pipeline/README.md).

Benchmarks audited. ACEBench, BFCL v3 and v4, ComplexFuncBench, tau-bench and tau2-bench, DrafterBench, NexusBench (src: pipeline/README.md); the runner additionally wraps MultiChallenge, for 11 runnable configs including separate BFCL prompting and function-calling modes (src: README). The corresponding harnesses (gorilla, tau-bench, tau2-bench, ACEBench, …) are vendored in the repo tree.

Cleaned variants. Flagged tasks live in per-benchmark <bench>_issues.csv manifests feeding two variants: Filtered drops flagged tasks and re-scores existing results with no reruns (the launch variant); Fixed applies targeted repairs on fixed data and drops unfixable tasks (src: README). Repo-documented filtered task counts: ACEBench 1023 to 999, ComplexFuncBench 1000 to 871, tau-bench airline 50 to 39 and retail 115 to 109, tau2-bench airline 50 to 31, retail 114 to 102, telecom 114 to 104 (README) — flag rates from roughly 2% of a suite up to 38% of a split. DrafterBench issues found by the audit were reported upstream and fixed there (README), so no additional filtering is applied to it.

Caveats

  • Paper text unavailable — this entire summary is repo-based (see the prominent caveat at top): the issue taxonomy’s actual categories, the human-verification protocol, the pipeline’s precision/recall values, and whether/how model rankings change between Original and Filtered are all “Not reported”.
  • ICML 2026 acceptance unverified: treat as “presented/found at ICML 2026, acceptance status unverified”.
  • The repo itself scopes the fix: cleaned variants address “identifiable and verifiable issues” but “do not attempt to exhaustively correct all possible sources of noise” (src: README).
  • Audit validity hinges on the LLM judge and the human-labeled ground truth; the repo names the alignment metrics but not their values, and Fixed variants are unsupported for NexusBench/MultiChallenge (src: README, pipeline/README.md).
  • After upgrading with the PDF: what the component taxonomy actually is, the audit’s precision/recall vs humans, and whether Filtered scores reorder current frontier agents on the Hugging Face leaderboard.
